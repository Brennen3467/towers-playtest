# Lane 01: M&A Deal Origination (the seed idea)

*Research date: 2026-10-01. Research only: no outreach, signups or purchases were made. Every material claim has a URL. Anything marked **(est.)** is my own estimate, not a sourced figure. Many pricing figures come from vendor blogs or third-party aggregators rather than signed contracts, and they are flagged where that matters.*

---

## TL;DR (read this if nothing else)

- **The seed idea as stated is a red ocean.** "Agents research owners who fit a buyer's thesis and send personalized 'would you sell?' emails" is now a commodity service. Well-funded consolidators sell it: SourceCo bought CAPTARGET in April 2026 ([SourceCo](https://www.sourcecodeals.com/blog/sourceco-acquired-captarget-what-that-means-for-deal-sourcing)), and Datasite bought both Grata and Sourcescrub in 2025 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/datasite-to-acquire-sourcescrub-expanding-private-market-intelligence-solutions)). It is also sold by a long tail of cold-email agencies (Hypergen, Danish Lead Co, Axia and others) that charge **$2K–$25K/month**. Owners already report a "huge uptick" in unsolicited PE outreach ([HBK CPA](https://hbkcpa.com/insights/selling-your-business-a-private-equity-firm-might-want-to-buy-it/)).
- **People clearly pay.** Retainers run $2K–$25K/month, pay-per-meeting runs $300–$600, and success fees run 1–5% ([Axia](https://axiagrowth.com/blog/in-house-bdr-vs-outsourced-deal-sourcing), [CAPTARGET](https://www.captarget.com/insights/deal-origination-cost-comparison), [Raises/Axial](https://raises.com/blog/axial-pricing-membership-cost-2026)). Willingness to pay is not the problem. **Differentiation and deliverability are.**
- **Legal.** A **flat-fee** meeting or data service that never negotiates, values, or takes success-contingent pay is the conservative structure. Success fees are federally exempt only for "M&A brokers" under Exchange Act §15(b)(13), and that exemption does **not** pre-empt states; only ~23 states have aligned ([IBBA](https://www.ibba.org/articles/the-state-line-trap/)). Contracts with unregistered brokers can be voidable under §29(b), so you may not be able to collect your fee.
- **Where an edge might exist:** **vertical, public-data "succession-likelihood" scoring for licensed-trade and other long-tail SMBs** that horizontal databases cover poorly. The inputs are state license tenure, SOS formation dates, PPP job counts, SBA 7(a) ownership-change history and website staleness. That last signal is the brothers' existing core competency. Sell it as a **recurring data and target-map subscription to roll-up platforms**, with optional flat-fee outreach on top, rather than as yet another AI-SDR agency.
- **Top pick:** Idea #1 (Vertical Roll-up Target Intelligence), overall **6.4/10**, confidence **medium-low**. Nothing in this lane scores above 7. Unless the moat is the data combination plus vertical depth, this lane is a services business with agency margins and agency churn.

### 1.3 The supply: the owner-succession wave

- **2.9M** US employer businesses are owned by people 55+. They employ 32.1M people and generate $6.5T in revenue ([Project Equity](https://project-equity.org/news/employee-ownership-insider/business-owner-statistics-exit-planning/)).
- 73–75% of owners want to exit within 10 years, but only 32% have a documented exit plan ([EPI 2023](https://s3.amazonaws.com/static.contentres.com/media/documents/3c618ea5-556f-4d50-9bd7-f45aab4c2a91.pdf)).
- Baby Boomers are the majority of sellers. Retirement is the top reason for selling, cited for 38% of deals under $500K and 55% of $5–50M deals ([IBBA Market Pulse Q2 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-ibba-and-ma-source-release-the-market-pulse-q2-2025-survey-302523561.html)).
- **Reality check:** BizBuySell recorded only **9,586** closed small-business deals in 2025, at a $350K median ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/bizbuysell-2025-fourth-quarter-insight-report/)). The "silver tsunami" converts into closed sales slowly. Most owners who say "within 10 years" are not sellers this quarter, so outreach conversion stays low however good the targeting is.

### 1.5 Legal frame (not legal advice; budget for one securities-counsel review, est. $3–10K)

1. **Selling a company's stock is a securities transaction** (*Landreth Timber*, 1985). Taking transaction-based compensation for it historically required broker-dealer registration ([SEC 2014 letter](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/marketreg/mr-noaction/2014/ma-brokers-013114.pdf)). Pure asset sales do not trigger the federal rule ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/insights/legal/new-exemption-from-federal-broker-dealer-registration-for-manda-brokers)).
2. **The federal M&A broker exemption, Exchange Act §15(b)(13),** took effect 2023-03-29, when the 2014 no-action letter was withdrawn. It covers deals where the target has <$25M EBITDA and/or <$250M revenue and is not SEC-reporting, and where control transfers. The broker may not hold funds, arrange financing without disclosure, represent both sides without written consent, or form buyer groups. **It does not pre-empt state registration** ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2023/03/us-congress-exempts-certain-merger-and-acquisition-brokers-from-sec-broker-dealer-registration), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/securities/1824868/the-ma-broker-exemption)).
3. **States:** NASAA updated its model rule in May 2024, and ~23 states have some M&A exemption, with inconsistent thresholds and notice filings. Florida, California and Texas may require a **real estate license** for business brokerage involving real estate or lease assignments. Under §29(b) and state blue-sky analogues, **a contract made in violation can be void, so your fee can be clawed back** ([IBBA "State-Line Trap"](https://www.ibba.org/articles/the-state-line-trap/), [Vicente](https://vicentellp.com/insights/broker-dealers-finders-m-and-a-financings-frequently-asked-questions/)).
4. **Finders:** there is still no general federal finder exemption. The 2020 SEC proposal was never adopted ([PSBP Law](https://psbplaw.com/blog/sec-proposed-rule-for-finders/)). The *Paul Anka* (1991) letter is the usual guide: provide names only, no negotiation, no solicitation of the deal itself ([Anka letter](https://securities.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/anka.pdf)). California has a narrow finder statute (§25206.1) ([Fell](https://www.fellplaw.com/post/understanding-the-differences-between-broker-dealers-and-finders-in-california)).
5. **Safe-ish structure for the brothers:** a fixed monthly subscription (data, research, outreach on the client's behalf, meeting-setting) that does **not** vary with whether a deal closes. Outreach is sent **in the buyer's name or clearly identifies whom you represent**. You do not discuss valuation or terms and you never touch funds. Optionally, partner with a registered or exempt M&A broker for any success-fee upside. Pay-per-meeting is probably fine, since meetings are not transactions, but get counsel to bless it.
6. **Outreach law.** CAN-SPAM applies to B2B email with no exception, at up to **$53,088 per non-compliant email** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), [penalty](https://fearlessnirbhay.com/articles/can-spam-act-violation-penalty-53088-per-email/)). Gmail and Yahoo block senders above 0.3% spam complaints, and cold outreach routinely hits 0.5–1% ([Mailgun](https://www.mailgun.com/state-of-email-deliverability/chapter/yahoogle-bulk-senders/), [Puzzle Inbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-bulk-sender-requirements-2024)). The **TCPA** covers calls and SMS, and AI-voice calls count as "artificial voice," which requires prior express consent ([FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-400393A1.pdf)). **CCPA** covers B2B contact data since 2023-01-01 ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2023/01/january-1-2023-expiration-of-employee-and-b2b)). GDPR matters only if the brothers target EU/UK owners, so they should not do that at first.

### 1.6 Incumbents and gaps

- **Horizontal data:** Grata plus Sourcescrub (now one Datasite product), PitchBook, Inven, Cyndx. They are strong on $5M+ revenue companies with web footprints and **weak on owner-operator trades and businesses with stale or no websites**. That weakness is exactly the brothers' existing hunting ground.
- **Origination services:** SourceCo+CAPTARGET (1,500+ PE clients historically, 7,843 leads in 12 months), Hypergen, Axia, Danish Lead Co, plus hundreds of AI-SDR shops. **Crowded.**
- **Marketplaces:** Axial (success fee), the SourceCo marketplace, Kumo (aggregates 100K+ broker listings at $30–149/mo, a cheap anchor for on-market deals) ([Kumo](https://www.withkumo.com/pricing), [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/-140)), and DealSeam (buyer-pays success fee; publishes roll-up trackers) ([DealSeam](https://dealseam.com/hvac-pe-rollup-tracker-2026)).
- **AI-native banks:** OffDeal (YC; $17M raised; $10–100M revenue sell-side; 30+ mandates launched; targets $100M run-rate by 2027) ([OffDeal](https://offdeal.io/news/offdeal-series-a-funding-radical-ventures-2025), [EQT Thinq](https://eqtgroup.com/thinq/technology/first-ai-native-private-equity-firm)).
- **Gaps:**
  - (a) Vertical and licensed-trade depth, built from public-record joins rather than web-crawled firmographics.
  - (b) A **succession-likelihood score**, as opposed to a mere "fits thesis" match.
  - (c) Pricing for the long tail (independent sponsors, family offices, small platforms) between Kumo's $30/mo and Grata's $15K+/yr.
  - (d) Outreach that is non-generic because it contains *specific* facts: "your license was issued in 1989; your three techs…"

## 2. Candidate ideas

### Idea 1: Vertical Roll-up Target Intelligence ("SuccessionMap")

**Pitch:** a per-vertical, per-state subscription that maps every owner-operated business in a roll-up-heavy trade, scores each one for succession likelihood using public records, and refreshes monthly. Flat-fee outreach is an optional add-on.

- **Data sources:**
  - State license files: TX TDLR open data (free, [link](https://data.texas.gov/dataset/TDLR-All-Licenses/7358-krk7)); other states via portals or FOIA.
  - SOS entity data (free to ~$24K/yr per state).
  - PPP FOIA (free) and SBA 7(a) FOIA (free, quarterly).
  - UCC bulk (state-priced).
  - A website-staleness crawl (in-house).
  - Google Business Profile reviews via the official Places API. The API terms limit caching, so store only derived signals.
  - Data Axle owner contact append (~$0.07–$0.30/record).
  - Avoid LinkedIn scraping and voter files.
- **The new-value combination:**
  - License tenure (≥25 yrs ≈ owner 55+) × licensee headcount and PPP jobs (size band) × SOS officer names (owner identity, family succession) × SBA 7(a) history (bought with SBA ~8–12 yrs ago, or loan now paid in full) × website staleness and review-response decay (disengagement) × absence from any roll-up tracker (not already sold).
  - Horizontal databases do not join these sources. DealSeam publishes only who is *buying*. Everything is shown as **one score with the evidence behind it**, so a corp-dev associate can trust it.
- **Buyer persona:** VP of Corp Dev, or the "Head of M&A / Integration," at a PE-backed HVAC, plumbing or electrical platform (22 named HVAC consolidators ([DealSeam](https://dealseam.com/hvac-pe-rollup-tracker-2026)); ~800 HVAC, plumbing and electrical companies acquired since 2022). Also an operating partner at an LMM fund with a home-services thesis, and independent sponsors targeting trades.
- **Willingness to pay:**
  - Apex Service Partners closed ~60 add-ons in 2025 ([Hardwire](https://www.thehardwirenews.com/apex-service-partners-hvac-rollup-2026/)).
  - Platforms already pay $15K–$155K/yr for Grata-class data and $2K–$8K/mo for origination.
  - One in-house BD hire costs $180–220K over 18 months ([Axia](https://axiagrowth.com/blog/in-house-bdr-vs-outsourced-deal-sourcing)).
  - A single add-on is typically 4–7× EBITDA, and buyers pay finder fees above $240K (CAPTARGET survey).
- **Market size:**
  - **Bottom-up (est.):** roll-up-heavy verticals with licensable or registrable owner populations include HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pest, roofing, landscaping, fire and life safety, dental, vet, physical therapy, insurance agencies, MSPs, accounting and funeral homes. That is ~15 verticals × ~20–60 active consolidators each, or ~500–900 platforms, plus ~1,500 independent sponsors and ~3,180 family offices, a minority of which have trade theses.
  - Realistic SAM: ~600–1,200 paying accounts × $24–60K/yr ≈ **$15–70M/yr (est.)**. That is enough for a $1–5M ARR business, not a venture outcome.
  - Supporting counts: 130 PE-backed DSOs ([Becker's / search](https://www.beckersdental.com/dso-dpms/200-dso-affiliations-in-2025-state-by-state-breakdown/)); 466 MSP/MSSP deals in 2025 with PE at 69% ([Omdia / search](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/om142465/msp-ma-global-activity-for-2025)); ~50% of vet clinics corporate-owned ([CT Acquisitions / AVMA via search](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/private-equity-veterinary-2026/)).
- **Deliverable and pricing:**
  - Web dashboard plus CSV/CRM sync (DealCloud, HubSpot), refreshed monthly.
  - Vertical × state bundle at **$1,500–$3,000/mo**, national vertical at **$5–8K/mo**, annual contract.
  - Add-on: managed outreach at **$3–5K/mo flat**, no success fee.
  - Recurring: **yes**. Data decays, and platforms acquire continuously.
- **Automation pipeline:**
  1. *Find prospects (buyers):* scrape public roll-up announcements, press releases and PE portfolio pages to list platforms per vertical. Claude classifies each platform's thesis and geography.
  2. *Build deliverable:* ETL the license, SOS, PPP, SBA and UCC files. Claude does entity resolution (license holder ↔ SOS entity ↔ PPP borrower), scoring, and an evidence summary per target.
  3. *Personalized outreach:* email the platform's corp-dev lead a **free sample**: "the 25 highest-scored non-acquired HVAC contractors inside your current service radius." This is the brothers' "build first, then email" playbook transplanted.
  4. *Handle replies:* a Claude agent answers questions, books a demo and sends an annual order form.
  5. *Deliver and renew:* monthly refresh, a "new signals" digest, and usage-based renewal nudges.
- **% automatable:** ~80–85% (est.). The remaining human touchpoints are demo calls with PE buyers (they expect a person), entity-resolution QA on edge cases, state data procurement and contracting.
- **GTM, first 90 days:**
  - Days 1–20: build TX HVAC, plumbing and electrical with TDLR + PPP + SBA + SOS + staleness. Hand-validate 100 records.
  - Days 21–45: build free target lists for the 22 HVAC platforms and the ~20 most active plumbing and electrical consolidators, then outreach (≈60 emails).
  - Days 46–90: convert 3–5 design partners at a 50% discount. Add FL and AZ.
  - Kill gate: fewer than 2 paid pilots by day 90.
- **Unit economics (est.):**
  - CAC $1.5–4K (founder-led outbound to a list of a few hundred buyers).
  - ACV $24–48K.
  - Data cost <$500/mo per vertical-state, amortized across clients, since the data is non-exclusive.
  - Claude/compute ~$100–300 per client per month.
  - Gross margin **80–90% for data-only**, ~60–70% with managed outreach.
  - Payback in 1–3 months if renewals hold.
- **Competitors and crowding:**
  - Grata/Sourcescrub (Datasite): horizontal, so a trade-level, succession-scored product is **not** their focus, but they could build it.
  - DealSeam publishes trackers but sells success-fee intros to sellers' buyers.
  - Vertical brokers (Transitions Elite for vet, dental brokers).
  - Crowding: **medium** for the data product, **high** if it slides into outreach.
- **Legal and regulatory:** pure data and research, so no broker issue. Comply with each state's open-data terms. CCPA applies if California owners are profiled, so honor deletion requests. If outreach is added, see CAN-SPAM/TCPA above.
- **Kill risks:**
  1. Platforms say "our BD team already knows every shop in our radius." Vertical roll-ups often do; verify with 10 interviews first.
  2. The score does not predict sale willingness better than tenure alone, which would leave thin differentiation.
  3. Small TAM per vertical plus Datasite or DealSeam copying the feature.

### Idea 2: Flat-fee, thesis-matched add-on origination for PE platforms (the seed idea, done carefully)

**Pitch:** a fixed monthly fee for a managed program that delivers 4–10 qualified owner conversations per month with founder-owned add-on targets that match a platform's thesis, with every email grounded in public-record specifics.

- **Data sources:** those of Idea 1, plus web, news, Google reviews (via API), Data Axle contact append and the client's own exclusion lists. Licensed data only.
- **The combination:** succession score × thesis fit (Claude reads the platform's acquisition criteria and past add-ons) × hyper-specific personalization drawn from public facts.
- **Buyer persona:** a principal or VP at an LMM PE fund running buy-and-build, or an independent sponsor with a capital partner.
- **Willingness to pay:** CAPTARGET's average is $2.5K/mo for 4–8 meetings ([CAPTARGET](https://www.captarget.com/insights/how-compare-other-deal-origination-services)); SourceCo $4–8K/mo plus a close fee; Axia $350/meeting; programs run $10–25K/mo ([Axia](https://axiagrowth.com/blog/in-house-bdr-vs-outsourced-deal-sourcing)). CAPTARGET reportedly served 1,500+ PE firms over 15 years.
- **Market size:** add-ons are 72.9% of buyouts. With ~5,000 PE firms (est.) + ~1,500 independent sponsors + ~2,400 direct-investing family offices, perhaps 2,000–3,000 run active add-on or acquisition programs (est.). If 15% outsource, that is 300–450 × $36–60K/yr ≈ **$11–27M (est.)** for a flat-fee niche player.
- **Deliverable and pricing:** $3–6K/mo flat, or $400–600 per held meeting with a minimum. A 3-month minimum, then month-to-month. Recurring, but churn spikes when the client goes under LOI or pauses.
- **Automation pipeline:**
  - *Find buyers:* platform and add-on announcements (PR Newswire, PitchBook news) give "who is actively buying in vertical X."
  - *Build deliverable:* a sample target list plus 3 drafted owner emails written in the buyer's voice.
  - *Outreach to buyers:* a personalized email showing the sample.
  - *Owner outreach:* Claude drafts each email, citing tenure, service area and review themes, from client-branded domains. Deliverability ops (warming, domain rotation, complaint monitoring) stay below the 0.1% target.
  - *Replies:* Claude triages by intent (interested, not now (re-engage in 6–12 mo), angry (suppress)) and books owner calls on the buyer's calendar.
  - *Renewal:* a monthly pipeline report.
- **% automatable:** ~65–75% (est.). Humans handle client onboarding and thesis calibration, owner phone calls (owners often want a human voice before a meeting; AI voice needs consent under the TCPA) and deliverability firefighting.
- **GTM, 90 days:** pick one vertical (e.g. HVAC, or MSPs) and pitch 50 platforms with a free list of 25 targets. Goal: 3 paying clients by day 60 and 6 by day 90.
- **Unit economics (est.):**
  - CAC $2–5K.
  - Price $4K/mo.
  - Delivery cost: data ~$300/mo, sending infrastructure ~$200–400/mo per client, Claude ~$200/mo, ~10–15 human hrs/mo (calls, QA) at $40–60/hr ≈ $600–900.
  - Gross margin ~65–75%. Average lifetime is probably 6–9 months (agency norms; est.), so LTV ≈ $20–30K.
- **Competitors:** SourceCo+CAPTARGET, Hypergen, Axia, Danish Lead Co, Inven (positions itself as an origination platform), plus hundreds of AI-SDR shops. **Very crowded.**
- **Legal:** stay flat-fee, send in the buyer's name with disclosure of your agency role, and do not negotiate. CAN-SPAM, TCPA, CCPA apply. **Never** claim to be the buyer if you are not; that is deceptive under FTC Act §5 (an inference from general deceptive-practice law; get counsel).
- **Kill risks:**
  1. Owner fatigue: owners already get weekly PE pitches ([HBK](https://hbkcpa.com/insights/selling-your-business-a-private-equity-firm-might-want-to-buy-it/)), and reply rates sit around 1–3% ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/cold-email-templates-for-acquisition-outreach/)).
  2. Deliverability collapse under Gmail/Yahoo rules.
  3. Commodity pricing pressure from SourceCo's scale and AI-SDR shops; clients churn when they see "meetings" that are not sellers.

### Idea 3: Sell-side mandate generation for business brokers and LMM M&A advisors

**Pitch:** brokers and boutique banks pay a flat monthly fee to receive conversations with owners who are likely to sell in the next 6–24 months in their territory and size band.

- **Data:** the same succession score, by geography rather than vertical. Owner contact via Data Axle.
- **The combination:** succession score × the broker's deal-size band (PPP jobs as a revenue proxy) × geography. Outreach offers a **free valuation range or exit-readiness snapshot**, a real deliverable that mirrors the brothers' "build the site first" move.
- **Buyer persona:** principal of a 1–10-person brokerage (IBBA/M&A Source member) or an LMM sell-side boutique.
- **Willingness to pay:**
  - Hypergen's M&A service starts at $4,500/mo and claims advisors close 4–8 deals/yr from it at $150–300K fees (vendor claim) ([Hypergen](https://www.hypergen.io/industry/investment-banks-m-a-advisory)).
  - BizBuySell sells per-lead seller leads ([FAQ](https://www.bizbuysell.com/seller-leads-broker-faq/)).
  - Low-end agencies charge $500–1,250/mo ([Business Brokerage Leads](https://www.businessbrokerageleads.com/pricing)).
  - But industry revenue is only $1.0B across ~11K brokers ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/business-brokers/4796/)), so the average broker has **thin** budgets.
- **Market size (est.):** ~3,000–5,000 brokers and boutiques who actively buy leads × $1.5–3K/mo ≈ **$54–180M theoretical**. Realistically the reachable payers who sustain spend are far fewer.
- **Deliverable and pricing:** $1.5–3K/mo per territory, exclusive per territory, so it is recurring and scarce.
- **Automation pipeline:** find brokers (IBBA directory, BizBuySell broker pages) → build a sample "40 likely sellers in your county" → personalized email to the broker → Claude reply handling → monthly owner campaigns in the broker's name, then hand-off of warm replies.
- **% automatable:** ~75% (est.). The broker does all owner calls, which is a feature.
- **GTM, 90 days:** offer territory exclusivity to the first broker per metro. Target 30 metros and 5 paying clients.
- **Unit economics (est.):** CAC $1–2K; price $2K/mo; gross margin ~70%. Churn is high: brokers judge results on signed engagements, and the lag is 6–12 months.
- **Competitors:** Hypergen, BizBuySell Seller Leads, a dozen broker-lead agencies, and OffDeal competing for the same owners. **Crowded.**
- **Legal:** low. You are marketing for the broker, but the broker's licensing (real estate license in FL/CA/TX) is their problem. Make sure outreach doesn't promise valuations you're unqualified for; label any figure as an "indicative range." CAN-SPAM/TCPA apply.
- **Kill risks:**
  1. Brokers are cheap and judge on closed deals that lag 6–12 months, so churn comes before proof.
  2. Owners respond better to referrals (CPA, banker) than to cold email.
  3. Saturation from AI-SDR shops selling the identical promise.

### Idea 4: Search-funder "Deal Desk"

**Pitch:** a sub-$1K/month thesis-tuned off-market pipeline for self-funded and traditional searchers: scored targets, drafted outreach and reply triage.

- **Data:** as Idea 1, in any industry.
- **Buyer:** an MBA searcher in months 1–24 of a search ([Stanford](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/2024-search-fund-study)).
- **Willingness to pay:** software and data budgets of $30–50K per 2 years ([Searcher Insights](https://searcherinsights.com/how-much-capital-should-i-raise-for-my-search-fund/)). Kumo anchors at $30–149/mo ([Kumo](https://www.withkumo.com/pricing)). Searchers sell DIY guides to each other for $99 ([Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/proprietary-deal-flow-lead-gen-resource)).
- **Market size (est.):** perhaps 1,000–2,000 active searchers (traditional + self-funded + ETA) × $6–12K/yr ≈ **$6–24M**.
- **Pricing:** $500–1,000/mo. Recurring by design until the searcher acquires, which by design means churn at 18–24 months.
- **% automatable:** ~85%, since searchers do their own calls.
- **GTM:** Searchfunder.com, ETA conferences, school ETA clubs. Cheap and well-defined.
- **Unit economics (est.):** CAC $300–800; price $750/mo; gross margin ~75%; LTV ~$9–13K.
- **Competitors:** Kumo, Grata's searcher pricing, BizScout, interns at $23–42/hr ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Search-Fund-Internships)), and DIY Clay/Instantly stacks. **Crowded at the low end.**
- **Legal:** low. Searchers send under their own name.
- **Kill risks:**
  1. A price-sensitive, tech-savvy DIY audience.
  2. Built-in churn, since a buyer who buys a business leaves.
  3. Searcher-on-searcher competition for the same owners, so outreach yields fall.

### Idea 5: Owner liquidity-event signals for wealth managers, CPAs and exit planners

**Pitch:** sell a monthly feed of "owners likely to sell in 12–36 months" to advisors who want the *pre-liquidity* relationship, with no securities transaction anywhere near it.

- **Data:** the same succession score, filtered to larger owners (PPP jobs ≥20).
- **The combination:** the score × a wealth estimate (owned property records) × local geography.
- **Buyer persona:** an RIA advisor or team focused on business owners, an EPI CEPA (8,000+ [EPI](https://exit-planning-institute.org/)) or a CPA firm's advisory partner.
- **Willingness to pay:** **the weak spot.** Aidentified starts at $49/mo and already alerts on 16 wealth events including liquidity events ([Aidentified](https://www.aidentified.com/finny-alternative)). WealthFeed occupies the same space. Advisors pay for *events*, but forward-looking predictions are a harder sell.
- **Market size (est.):** a large buyer pool (hundreds of thousands of advisors) but a low price (~$100–500/mo), which means SaaS-style high volume and CAC. That is not the brothers' model.
- **% automatable:** ~90%.
- **Competitors:** Aidentified, WealthFeed, RIA in a Box-type lead programs. **Moderately crowded, low price.**
- **Legal:** advisors face FINRA/SEC marketing rules on their own outreach, while the data seller faces CCPA.
- **Kill risks:**
  1. The $49/mo price anchor.
  2. Prediction lag: advisors want the event now.
  3. Weak attribution, since an advisor might wait 3 years for an AUM payoff.

### Idea 6: Strategic-acquirer "Tuck-in Radar" for PE-less mid-market companies

**Pitch:** a flat-fee research and outreach service for $20–300M-revenue private companies (distributors, regional service firms) that do 1–3 tuck-ins a year but have no corp-dev staff.

- **Data:** as Idea 1, plus the client's customer and supplier geographies.
- **Buyer persona:** the CEO or CFO of a family-owned or ESOP mid-market company.
- **Willingness to pay:** these companies hire buy-side advisors on retainers of ~$5K+/mo and pay 2–5% success fees ([CAPTARGET](https://www.captarget.com/insights/deal-origination-cost-comparison)). CAPTARGET publishes a strategic-buyer case study, which shows the demand exists ([CAPTARGET](https://www.captarget.com/insights/strategic-buyer-case-study)).
- **Market size (est.):** perhaps 5,000–15,000 acquisitive private strategics. They are **hard to identify**, though acquisition announcements and SBA/UCC lender changes help. ~1% adoption at $4K/mo is roughly $2.4–7M.
- **% automatable:** ~65%. Strategics need hand-holding.
- **GTM:** use the brothers' data to find companies that announced a tuck-in in the last 24 months. Bonus synergy: these companies likely also have dated websites, which ties into the brothers' existing business.
- **Unit economics (est.):** CAC $3–8K (longer sales cycle); price $3–5K/mo; gross margin ~70%.
- **Competitors:** buy-side boutiques, CAPTARGET/SourceCo. **Medium crowding.** The niche is underserved, but buyer discovery is expensive.
- **Legal:** same as Idea 2.
- **Kill risks:**
  1. The buyer is hard to find and slow to decide.
  2. Episodic need: strategics stop after one deal.
  3. CEOs prefer relationship-based bankers.

### Idea 7: AI-native micro sell-side bank (OffDeal clone, sub-$10M revenue)

**Pitch:** run sell-side processes for $1–10M-revenue owners. AI drafts the CIM, buyer lists and outreach, and earns a success fee.

- **Data:** the succession score for owner acquisition, plus the Idea 1 buyer graph.
- **WTP:** strong per deal: Double Lehman on a $3M deal ≈ $240K ([Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/ma-advisor-fees)).
- **Why it scores low for the brothers:**
  - **Success fees require** federal M&A-broker-exemption compliance *and* state registration or a real estate license in many states ([IBBA](https://www.ibba.org/articles/the-state-line-trap/)).
  - It is not recurring.
  - It is human-heavy: owners need hand-holding and diligence is hard. Clarum found confirmatory diligence still requires humans ([EQT Thinq](https://eqtgroup.com/thinq/technology/first-ai-native-private-equity-firm)).
  - It means head-on competition with VC-funded OffDeal ($17M raised) and 11K brokers.
- **% automatable:** ~40–50%.
- **Kill risks:** regulation and licensing, non-recurring revenue, and a 6–12-month time to first dollar.

## 3. Ideas considered and rejected

| Idea | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Success-fee finder for PE (no registration)** | §29(b) voidability plus state registration; ~27 states have no aligned exemption ([IBBA](https://www.ibba.org/articles/the-state-line-trap/)). The fee may be uncollectable. Survives only as Idea 2 (flat fee) or via a registered partner. |
| **AI-voice cold-calling owners at scale** | The FCC's Feb-2024 ruling treats AI voices as "artificial," so prior express consent is required ([FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-400393A1.pdf)). TCPA class-action exposure. |
| **Owner-age scoring from voter files** | 23 states forbid commercial use; in WA misuse is a Class C felony ([Ballotpedia summary](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/07/28/twenty-one-states-and-d-c-make-voter-files-openly-available/)). Use license tenure as the age proxy instead. |
| **LinkedIn-scraped tenure and age data** | hiQ outcome: breach of contract, permanent injunction, $500K ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn)). |
| **Horizontal private-company database (Grata competitor)** | Datasite consolidated Grata + Sourcescrub + Blueflame with $500M from CapVest ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/datasite-to-acquire-sourcescrub-expanding-private-market-intelligence-solutions)). Capital-intensive and unwinnable. |
| **Two-sided deal marketplace (Axial competitor)** | Network effects favor the incumbents (Axial, the SourceCo marketplace, BizBuySell). Success-fee legal issues. Cold start on both sides. |
| **Broken-deal / re-trade recycler** | Real (46% of independent sponsors have closed broken-auction deals ([Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/independent-sponsor-guide))), but the data is private and held by bankers. No access path. |
| **EU/UK owner outreach** | GDPR legitimate-interest balancing and PECR limits on B2B email to sole traders. Adds cost with no advantage. |
| **Generic "AI SDR for PE" agency (no data edge)** | This is the saturated core of the market: Hypergen, Danish Lead Co, Axia, Inboxsniper, Puzzle Inbox and others all publish PE-specific playbooks ([search results](https://danishleadco.io/blog/top-outbound-agencies-for-private-equity-ma-firms)). The average cold-email reply rate is 3.43% (Instantly benchmark cited by [puzzleinbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-for-private-equity/)). There is no moat. |

### Honest bottom line

- **Willingness to pay is proven, but the generic service is commoditized and consolidating.** SourceCo+CAPTARGET and Datasite (Grata+Sourcescrub) are rolling up the category, and AI-SDR shops undercut on price. Another "personalized would-you-sell emails" agency would enter at #2 with no moat and agency churn.
- **The only defensible angle I found is #1:** a **public-record join** (license tenure × SOS × PPP × SBA × UCC × website staleness) packaged as **vertical, succession-scored target maps**, sold as recurring data to the ~500–900 roll-up platforms. Outreach is an upsell, not the core.
  - It reuses the brothers' website-staleness tooling.
  - It avoids broker-dealer exposure entirely.
  - It works best where horizontal databases are weakest.
- **Cheapest validation (est. 3–4 weeks, <$1K):** build TX HVAC, plumbing and electrical from TDLR + PPP + SBA, back-test the score against the ~800 trades companies acquired since 2022 (do acquired shops score high?), then send 20 platforms a free 25-target sample.
  - Kill if the back-test lift over "license age alone" is under ~1.5×, or if fewer than 2 of 20 platforms take a paid pilot.
