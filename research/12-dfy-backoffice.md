# Lane 12: Deadline-driven back-office work, done for you by agents

*Round-2 research lane. Date: 2026-10-01. Research only: no outreach, signups or purchases. Every material claim has a URL. Where a source is a vendor blog or an SEO comparison page rather than a primary source, I say so. Where I could not confirm something, I mark it **unverified**.*

## TL;DR

- **Almost every deadline-driven back-office niche in the brief already has a cheap self-serve tool *and* a human done-for-you (DFY) service.** Lien notices cost $19–$65 each. COI tracking with licensed reviewers costs about $1 per vendor per month. Sales-tax filings cost $75. IFTA returns cost $24.95 a quarter. Registered-agent service costs $125 a year. The round-1 failure ("no competitor found") would repeat in every one of these, so I rejected them.
- **The only pattern that survived the first pass:** compliance work where (a) a fragmented market of *human consultants* already charges $100–$1,000+/month, (b) the work is mostly documents, forms and chasing, which agents can do, and (c) the buyer cannot simply buy a $50/month tool, because the tool still leaves them doing the work. Two niches fit this best:
  1. **Prequalification-platform upkeep (ISNetworld/Avetta/Veriforce)** for small industrial contractors. Consultants charge $100–$500/month for upkeep and $1k–$6k/month for full outsourcing. More than 200k contractor accounts exist across the platforms.
  2. **Prevailing-wage / certified-payroll desk** for micro subcontractors on public works (California first). Managed services charge $15–$50 per report and $750–$5,000/month. Gusto and QuickBooks Online cannot produce certified payroll reports, which leaves micro subs stuck.
- **Self-verification knocked both down.**
  - **Wellstanding** already sells a done-for-you certified-payroll desk for CA subs at **$995 setup plus $249/mo**. It covers WH-347, DIR eCPR XML, DAS-140/142 and human review, and the first filing is free ([Wellstanding](https://wellstanding.com/)). My draft had listed it as "pricing not found".
  - **Avetta sells its own done-for-you service ("Vetify")** to suppliers ([Avetta plans](https://www.avetta.com/suppliers-contractors/plans)).
  - QuickBooks Online Payroll Premium and Elite now produce the federal WH-347.
- **Final scores: 3–4.5/10. Nothing in this lane reaches 5.**
- **Recommendation:**
  - **Don't start a standalone cold-outbound back-office business in this lane.**
  - The one test worth running is **Idea 5**: a bundled "Contractor Back-Office" upsell to the brothers' *existing* trade customers. It costs about $0 and takes 30 days. It works only if their base includes commercial/public-works subs.
  - If attach is above 3%, add certified payroll (Idea 1) and prequal upkeep (Idea 2) as modules in the bundle. Don't sell them as standalone cold offers.

### 1.2 Labor being replaced (quantified)

- **Certified payroll specialist (CA):** $27–$35/hr, about $56k–$73k/yr before burden. Prevailing-wage payroll specialists reach $42–$51/hr ([ZipRecruiter CA](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Certified-Payroll/--in-California)). A small sub doesn't hire one. The owner's spouse or bookkeeper does it, or nobody does.
- **Full-time AR clerk:** $45k–$65k salary, $60k–$85k loaded ([Axim](https://aximinc.com/accounts-receivable-outsourcing-costs/)).
- **Prequalification paperwork:** Avetta onboarding takes 40–80 hours of admin labor, and written safety programs cost $2k–$10k if outsourced ([SafetyPro via search summary](https://www.safetyproresources.com/blog/what-does-avetta-compliance-cost); [CrewCompliance](https://crewcompliance.org/isnetworld-ravs-safety-programs/)).
- **Offshore construction VA:** $8/hr ([VirtualNexGen](https://virtualnexgen.com/blog/construction-virtual-assistant-guide-2026)). US managed VA: $1,999–$5,400/mo full-time ([VA Masters](https://vamasters.com/virtual-assistant-services-that-drive-results/)). **This is the real price ceiling for any agent-run desk.** A contractor can rent a human who does all of this for $1,400/month.

### 1.3 Where the incumbents leave a gap

Incumbents split into two groups:
- **Software ($49–$300/mo).** Cheap, but the buyer still does the work.
- **Human consultancies ($750–$6,000/mo).** Do the work, but are priced and staffed for 25+ employee firms. Points North, for example, charges a $2,500 setup and about $16.9k in year one ([CertifiedPayrollPro comparison, a competitor's page](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/certified-payroll-services)).

The gap is **DFY at $150–$400/mo for 1–20 employee firms**. Agents make that price point profitable. That is the only structural opening I found, and AI-native entrants are already moving into it (Trayd, Dili, EHS Inc, Permitio, FlowManual: [YC construction cohort](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/engineering-and-construction/ycs-summer-2026-cohort-floods-construction-and-proptech-with-ai-back-office-tools)).

### 1.4 Cold-email reply-rate baseline

- Overall 2026 B2B average reply rate: **3.43%**. Top quartile 5.5%, elite 10%+ ([Instantly 2026 benchmark](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)).
- Construction/real estate: median **5.0%**, top decile 9.1%. Trigger-led outreach (e.g. a permit filed) is claimed to roughly triple replies ([The Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)). Vendor-published, so treat as optimistic.
- Another dataset puts construction far lower, at 0.56–0.60% ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-benchmarks-by-industry)). **The spread is wide.** I plan on 2–4% total replies and 0.5–1.5% positive replies.

## 2. Candidate ideas

### Idea 1: Prevailing-Wage Desk: certified payroll and apprentice paperwork for micro public-works subcontractors (California first)

**Pitch.** "You won a public job. We'll do your weekly certified payroll, DIR eCPR uploads, DAS-140/142 apprentice notices and fringe statements from your Gusto or QuickBooks export, for one flat fee. You sign; we do everything else."

**Why the gap exists.**
- Gusto does not generate WH-347 or handle prevailing wage ([CertifiedPayrollPro on Gusto](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/for/gusto)); this is third-party sourced.
- **Correction from verification:** QuickBooks Online Payroll Premium and Elite *do* now produce a federal WH-347 through QuickBooks Workforce. They don't produce CA eCPR XML ([Intuit](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/payroll-reports/create-review-certified-payroll-report-quickbooks/L5mWL9ujT_US_en_US)). Paychex Flex also produces WH-347 ([Dapt](https://www.dapt.tech/blog/paychex-certified-payroll)).
- So the gap is mainly **CA eCPR plus DAS paperwork**, not the WH-347 itself.
- So a 6-person electrical sub on Gusto that wins a school-district job has to buy a new tool *and* learn classifications, fringe crediting and eCPR XML. Or it hires a bookkeeper.

**Data and inputs; access.**
- **Client inputs:** the payroll register export and timesheets by project.
- **Public inputs:** DIR prevailing-wage determinations; DIR eCPR (XML upload, at least monthly, usually weekly) ([Trayd guide](https://www.buildtrayd.com/blog/california-dir-payroll-compliance-for-public-works); [DIR eCPR user guide](https://www.dir.ca.gov/public-works/ecpruserguide.pdf)).
- **Lead data:**
  - The **DIR Public Works Contractor Registration search** is filterable by registration dates, county and city. Entering "%" returns all registrants, and results can be exported ([DIR PWCR guide](https://www.dir.ca.gov/public-works/PWCR-Guide-External-6.2023.pdf); [Siskiyous summary](https://www.siskiyous.edu/maintenance/documents/DIR_publicworks.pdf)). Registration costs $400 and renews by June 30 ([ABC SoCal](https://abcsocal.org/dir-registration-for-california-public-works-contractors-2026-compliance-guide/)).
  - The **Caltrans labor-compliance annual report** names contractors with "Notice of Withholding" for "Failure to submit certified payroll records" and underpayments (FY 2025-26 PDF, which I parsed: [Caltrans](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/construction/documents/labor-compliance/reports/2026_dir_annual_report.pdf)).
  - **PWC-100 project records** list awarding bodies and projects ([DIR PWC-100](https://www.dir.ca.gov/PWC100Ext/ProjectWelcomePage.aspx)).
- **Data quality:** whether the PWCR export includes email or phone is **unverified**. The registration form collects contact data, but I could not confirm the public export shows it.

**Buyer.** CA licensed subs with 1–20 field workers, doing their first or occasional public works, who run payroll on Gusto, QBO or ADP RUN.

A second buyer is the **small GC**:
- California makes the prime liable for subs' wage and apprentice failures. In one case a GC was penalized for a sub's failure to hire apprentices ([Cal Construction Law Blog](https://calconstructionlawblog.com/2024/09/16/general-contractor-gets-fired-upon-for-subcontractors-failure-to-hire-apprentices/)).
- GCs could require or sponsor the desk for their subs.

**Evidence money moves today.**
- Managed certified payroll runs $1,000–$5,000/month, or $15–$50 per report plus a base fee. Points North's example: $750/mo base + $25/report + $2,500 setup ≈ $16.9k in year one ([CertifiedPayrollPro comparison](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/certified-payroll-services); competitor-authored, so treat the numbers as indicative).
- eBacon can run $20k+/yr for 25 people; Points North quotes $175–$300/mo plus $995–$4,995 setup; Payroll4Construction runs about $300–$600/mo for 10–25 employees ([CertifiedPayrollPro alternatives page](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/alternatives); [Capterra P4C](https://www.capterra.com/p/276449/Payroll4Construction/)).
- Local DFY firms exist: One2S Compliance (LA) audits timesheets and prepares CPRs weekly ([One2S](https://one2scompliance.com/services/certified-payroll)).
- Penalties are real:
  - $100 per worker per day if CPRs aren't produced within 10 days of a request, collected by withholding progress payments ([Miter](https://www.miter.com/resources/certified-payroll-california/); [ABC SoCal](https://abcsocal.org/prevailing-wage-certified-payroll-in-california-penalties-pitfalls-and-how-southern-california-contractors-stay-compliant/)).
  - Up to $100–$300 per day for apprentice violations under LC §1777.7 ([FindLaw text](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-1777-7.html)).
  - A "$45.2M in DIR penalties in 2025" figure appeared only in search summaries, so I dropped it as **unverified**.

**Market size (bottom-up).**
- About 21,000 contractors were registered in FY2014-15. That figure comes from a search-engine summary of DIR material; I could not open the underlying page, so treat it as **unverified**. The current count is also **unverified**; assume 25–40k.
- Assume 30–40% are 1–20 worker subs without construction payroll software: about 9–14k. Assume 20% have an active public job in a given month and no current solution: about 2–3k reachable buyers in CA.
- At $350/mo, the CA serviceable market is about **$8–$12M/yr**.
- Adding other states with heavy prevailing-wage reporting (WA intents/affidavits, NJ, NY, IL, MA) and federal Davis-Bacon (WH-347) gives perhaps 3–4x that.
- Realistic year-1 capture: 40–120 clients, or **$170k–$500k ARR**.

**Deliverable and pricing (recurring).**
- $349/mo covers one active project (weekly CPRs, eCPR upload, DAS-140 within 10 days of subcontract, DAS-142 dispatch requests, a monthly fringe/benefit statement).
- Each extra concurrent project costs $99/mo. Setup is $0.
- Optional GC-sponsored plan: $199 per sub per month, billed to the GC.
- Months without an active job are free, which means revenue is **lumpy** because it follows the jobs.

**Automation pipeline.**
| Step | What agents do | % automatable | Human touchpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find | Pull new PWCR registrations (date filter), cross-reference CSLB licence class and website, plus Caltrans withholding lists; enrich email | 85% | Spot-check enrichment |
| Build deliverable | Pre-built sample CPR for the prospect's trade and county ("here's what your week-1 CPR looks like for a Group 2 electrician in Fresno"), with the applicable wage determination attached | 90% | None |
| Outreach | Personalized email: registration date, trade, county wage rate, DAS-140 10-day rule | 95% | None |
| Replies | Agent answers FAQs and books onboarding | 70% | Pricing objections; a 15-min call for skeptics |
| Deliver | Parse payroll export, map workers to classifications, compute fringe credit, generate the eCPR XML and the DAS forms | 75–85% | **Owner signs** the statement of compliance (A-1-131) under penalty of perjury; a human handles **classification judgment calls** (e.g. Laborer vs. Operator) and DIR portal edge cases |
| Renew | Auto-continues while jobs are active; re-engages at each new award | 90% | None |

**90-day go-to-market.**
- Weeks 1–2: build the classification and wage-determination engine for 5 trades in 10 counties; scrape the PWCR registration list.
- Weeks 3–6: email 1,500 new registrants plus 300 subs on Caltrans withholding lists.
- Weeks 6–12: hand-deliver the first 10 clients with a human reviewer and a 48-hour SLA. Pitch 30 small GCs on the sponsored plan.

**Unit economics.**
- Revenue: $349/mo.
- Costs per client: LLM and compute about $15/mo; human review about 1.5 hrs/mo at $40 = $60; errors and omissions (E&O) insurance about $10/client/mo (assumes an $8–12k/yr policy spread over 100 clients, **unverified quote**).
- Gross margin: about **75%**.
- CAC: at 1% positive reply and 25% close, 400 emails per client. That is about $40 in sending infrastructure plus about $60 of human sales time, so roughly $100.
- Retention is tied to job duration: on average, a 4–8 month job plus about 1.5 more jobs a year, so LTV is about $2.5–$4k.

**The honest read (after verification):** the done-for-you gap I claimed is **already filled**. Wellstanding sells nearly the same desk at $249/mo, below my $349, and markets it with county-level CA SEO pages. This repeats round-1 failure #1. Any entry would need a wedge: no setup fee, GC sponsorship, or bundling into Idea 5.

**Five required answers.**
- **Who pays today, and to whom?** Subs pay managed services ($750+/mo) or bookkeepers, or eat the penalties (Caltrans withholding notices exist).
- **Why not DIY or a $50/mo tool?** The tool still needs someone who understands classifications, fringe crediting, DAS-140 timing and eCPR. A 6-person sub's owner doesn't have that knowledge. **Counterpoint:** many will try the $49 tool first.
- **Is the trigger early enough?** Yes. A new PWCR registration comes *before* bidding or work, and DAS-140 is due within 10 days of the subcontract. Withholding-list subs are late, but each has a live, urgent problem.
- **Licensing:** none needed to prepare CPRs. The contractor signs the certification. Don't touch payroll funds, to avoid money-transmission rules. Carry E&O. Don't give legal advice on classification disputes; refer to a labor attorney.
- **Reply rate:** 3–5% total, 1–1.5% positive, if the trigger and the sample CPR are personalized.

**Kill risks.**
- (1) Trayd, CertifiedPayrollPro or LCPcertified's free first project makes "good enough" free.
- (2) Liability: a wrong classification means back wages plus penalties. An E&O claim could exceed a year's revenue from that client.
- (3) Lumpy revenue: subs churn between jobs.
- (4) Portal access is **mostly OK**. Other users can link to an active PWCR registration once it is approved from the business email ([PWCR guide](https://www.dir.ca.gov/public-works/PWCR-Guide-External-6.2023.pdf)). The contractor still signs each upload ([DIR upload guide](https://www.dir.ca.gov/Public-Works/Guides/Upload-eCPR.pdf)).
- (5) **Wellstanding at $249/mo already occupies the position.**

**Cheapest validation test.**
- Pull 400 PWCR registrations from the last 90 days, plus 100 contractors named in Caltrans withholding notices. Send a personalized email with a sample CPR. Offer a $349/mo founding price. Budget: about $300 and 3 weeks.
- **Kill if** fewer than 8 positive replies (2%), or fewer than 2 paid clients at ≥$249/mo by day 30.
- **Pivot to the GC-sponsored plan if** GCs reply at more than 2x the rate subs do.

### Idea 2: Prequal Desk: done-for-you ISNetworld / Avetta / Veriforce upkeep for small industrial contractors

**Pitch.** "Keep your ISNetworld grade at A and your Avetta status green across every client for $299/mo. We write and update your RAVS safety programs, answer client questionnaires, upload insurance and OSHA 300A data every year, and fix flags before your client sees them."

**Data and inputs; access.**
- **Client:** current safety manual, OSHA 300/300A logs, EMR letter, COIs, training records.
- **Platforms:** ISN's user agreement lets a company authorize unlimited users, including an **independent contractor working for the company**, each with a unique login ([ISN contractor user agreement](https://www.isnetworld.com/en/user-agreements/contractor-supplier)). Consultants must not imply affiliation with ISN ([ISN trademark policy](https://www.isnetworld.com/en/trademark-logo-policy)).
- **Lead finding:**
  - The brothers' existing website crawler can scan SMB sites for "ISNetworld", "Avetta", "Veriforce", "PEC" badges.
  - Hiring-client "contractor letters" and program pages are public (e.g. [Avetta MasTec page](https://pages.avetta.com/MasTec)).
  - Indeed job posts for "ISN coordinator" are a signal that a company is paying a human for this.

**Buyer.** Contractors with 5–100 employees serving refineries, utilities, manufacturers, telecom and pipelines: electrical, mechanical, scaffolding, insulation, industrial cleaning, trucking and hauling, staffing. Mainly TX, LA, OK, PA, OH, AB.

**Evidence money moves today.**
- **Platforms:** ISN costs $875/yr for the smallest contractors, $1,700–$5,000 for mid-sized, plus a $300–$850 setup ([CoverWarden](https://coverwarden.com/learn/isnetworld-cost); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/145737/ISNetworld/pricing/)). Avetta costs about $399–$1,299/yr for small and medium suppliers ([ExpiryEdge](https://expiryedge.com/articles/avetta-vs-isnetworld-comparison/)).
- **Consultants** ([SafetyPro Resources](https://www.safetyproresources.com/blog/what-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-good-isnetworld-consultant)):
  - Setup $800–$1,200.
  - Basic monitoring **$100–$300/mo**; enhanced **$250–$500/mo**.
  - Full outsourcing **$1,000–$6,000/mo**.
- **Per-program fixes:** $500–$2,000+ each; full written programs $2k–$10k ([CrewCompliance](https://crewcompliance.org/isnetworld-ravs-safety-programs/)).
- **Account-maintenance services** that upload COIs, renew documents, update OSHA records quarterly and annually, and create new programs for new client requirements are sold at an annual flat fee ([Industrial Compliance Safety](https://www.industrialcompliancesafety.com/isnetworld-account-maintenance/)).
- **M&A in the consultant market:** JobQualified was acquired by Irwin's Safety (May 2026) ([JobQualified](https://jobqualified.com/blogs/isnetworld/cost)).

**Market size (bottom-up).**
- Accounts: ISN has about 85–90k active contractors and suppliers (850–900 hiring clients). **These are global counts**, so the US/CA small-contractor slice is probably 30–60k ([ISN about](https://www.isnetworld.com/en/about-isn)). Avetta has about 125k+ contractors (450+ clients) ([Delta Wye / ISN figures via search](https://deltawye.com/isnetworld-vs-avetta/); [EHS Inc](https://ehs.inc/ehs/avetta-vs-isnetworld)). That is about 150k unique US/CA firms after overlap (my estimate).
- If 30% have fewer than 50 employees and no in-house safety coordinator (about 45k), and 10% would outsource at $300/mo, that is **about $16M/yr serviceable**.
- The existing consultant market is plausibly $50–$150M/yr. This is an **unverified** estimate.
- Year-1 realistic: 50–150 clients, or **$180k–$540k ARR**.

**Deliverable and pricing (recurring).**
- $750 onboarding ("get to grade A in 30 days", including drafting missing RAVS programs).
- $299/mo for one platform, $449/mo for multiple platforms.
- Recurring work every year:
  - Annual OSHA 300A statistics upload (due early in the year).
  - Annual renewal.
  - Insurance uploads at each policy renewal.
  - New client-specific questionnaires.
  - Program revisions when the platform changes a RAVS requirement.

**Automation pipeline.**
| Step | Agents | % | Human |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find | Crawl SMB sites for platform badges; parse Avetta client pages; job-post signals | 85% | QA list |
| Build deliverable | "Prequal health check": from public info (OSHA establishment search, website, the contractor's own published safety page), list the RAVS programs their trade usually needs and which typical hiring clients require them | 80% | None |
| Outreach | Personalized email naming the platforms they show and the likely program gaps | 95% | None |
| Replies | Book a 20-min intake | 70% | Sales call for larger accounts |

### Idea 3: Audit-Ready Desk: workers'-comp and GL premium-audit preparation for small contractors (Florida first)

**Pitch.** "Your workers' comp policy expires on [date]. The audit comes 30–90 days later. Every 1099 sub without a valid COI for the period gets added to *your* payroll at the highest rate. We'll match every sub payment to a coverage window, chase the missing certificates now, and hand your auditor a clean binder. $299, one time."

**Data and inputs; access.**
- **Florida DWC Proof of Coverage database:** all employers with policies reported in the past 5 years, searchable by expiration and effective date, county and construction flag, with Excel export. It also offers "policy data in batch format" ([FL DWC POC download](https://dwcdataportal.fldfs.com/proofofcoverage.aspx); [POC data portal](https://dwcdataportal.fldfs.com/POCData.aspx)). Results reportedly show **reported payroll and governing class code** ([WeShopInsurance, 2016](https://www.weshopinsurance.com/news/2016/03/how-do-i-make-sure-the-subcontractor-i-hire-has-a-valid-workers-comp-policy-or-valid-exemption)). **Verification found this unconfirmed:** the bulk downloads sit behind a CAPTCHA, their columns are undocumented, and there is no email or phone.
- **The same POC database** verifies whether each *subcontractor* had coverage, which is the core of the deliverable.
- **Client inputs:** 1099 and vendor payment ledger from QBO, plus COIs on file.

**Buyer.** Florida construction employers with 1–25 employees who use 1099 subs. Florida requires contractors to obtain evidence of subs' coverage ([FAC 69L-6.032](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-69L-6-032)).

**Evidence money moves today.**
- **Contingency consultants:** Advanced Insurance Management claims about $30M in reductions and refunds since 1987 and finds significant errors in one-third to one-half of cases reviewed ([cutcomp.com](https://www.cutcomp.com/dispute.htm)). Spread over 38 years, that is under $1M a year, **a thin market**.
- **Typical exposure:** a $50k payment to an uninsured construction sub can add about $3,900+ of premium ([CompAuditPrep guides via search](https://compauditprep.com/guides/)).
- **Funded or pre-launch SaaS:** CompAuditPrep $79/$149/$299 per month, "founding-member estimates" (i.e. pre-launch) ([pricing](https://compauditprep.com/pricing/)).
- **Weak WTP signal:** insurance agents and carriers help with audit prep for free ([LWCC guide](https://www.lwcc.com/resources/premium-audit-preparation-a-guide)).

**Market size (bottom-up).**
- FL has hundreds of thousands of construction employers. Assume about 40k with 1099 subs and policies under $50k. At a 2% annual take at $299 plus $49/mo upkeep, that is about **$0.6–1M/yr**. Small.

**Deliverable and pricing.** $299 per pre-audit binder (ledger-to-COI match, retroactive certificate chase, class-code and overtime-premium review), plus $49/mo continuous sub-COI collection. **The recurring part is weak.**

**Automation.**
- Find (95%): POC expirations in the next 60 days.
- Build (85%): exposure estimate from the payroll/class code plus a sample "uninsured sub" calculation.
- Outreach (95%).
- Deliver (75%): ledger parsing; agents email subs for certificates.
- Humans: class-code questions; anything that looks like advice on coverage.

**Competitors (8 searches).** CompAuditPrep ($79–$299, pre-launch); BCS (COI with licensed reviewers, ≈$0.95/vendor/mo); TrackMyVendor ($39); myCOI; Billy; cutcomp.com and workcompconsultant.com (contingency); Reliable Premium Management (audit reviews plus pay-as-you-go) ([RPM](https://reliablepremium.com/resources/workers-comp-audit-subcontractors-what-business-owners-must-know-before-filing/)); carriers' own audit services; pay-as-you-go WC through payroll providers, which makes audits smaller.

**Five required answers.**
- **Who pays today?** Mostly nobody. Agents help for free and contingency consultants work only big refunds.
- **Why not DIY or $50?** CompAuditPrep's $79 tool exists. The DFY value is the *chasing*.
- **Is the trigger early enough?** **Yes.** The expiration date is public before the audit. This is the best trigger timing in the lane.
- **Licensing:** preparing records is fine. *Disputing* an audit on the insured's behalf, or charging a percentage of premium saved, may count as acting as an insurance consultant or adviser in some states. **Flat fee, documents only; no advice on coverage.** The state-by-state rules are **unverified**.
- **Reply rate:** about 3%. Dollar-quantified exposure helps, but owners are skeptical of "your premium audit" mail. This channel is close to insurance-agent X-date marketing.

**Kill risks (after verification).**
- Florida DWC runs a **free Construction Policy Tracking Database**. It emails contractors when a listed sub's policy or exemption lapses ([FL DFS](https://secure.fldfs.com/wcapps/contractor/Logon.asp); [FUBA](https://fuba.org/2018/02/12/do-you-know-about-the-construction-policy-tracking-database-for-workers-compensation/)). That kills the $49/mo recurring piece.
- FS 624.10 defines "transact" to include matters "subsequent to effectuation of a contract… arising out of it". FS 626.015 reserves fee-based insurance advice for licensed unaffiliated agents. So class-code or audit "review" for a fee is a **licensing risk** (unlicensed transacting is a felony) ([624.10](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/624.10); [626.015](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2024/626.015)).
- WTP is anchored at free: agents and carriers publish audit kits and do pre-audit reviews.
- The work is mostly one-time.

**Cheapest validation.**
- Email 500 FL construction employers whose policies expire in the next 45 days, with a personalized exposure estimate.
- **Kill if** fewer than 5 paid binders at $299 by day 30.

### Idea 4: Get-Paid Desk for commercial subs and suppliers: prelims on every job, pre-delinquency follow-up, NOI, and retainage closeout packages

**Pitch.** "Only 11% of subs send a prelim on every job. We'll send yours on every job, follow up on every invoice at day 30/45/60, send the notice of intent on time, and assemble the closeout package that releases your retainage."

**Evidence.**
- Only 11% of survey respondents prelim every project ([Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/blog/why-subcontractors-dont-send-prelims-and-why-theyre-wrong/)).
- Slow payment cost construction $280B in 2024; the average invoice takes about 90 days to pay ([Rabbet via Finoya](https://www.finoya.ai/blog/progress-billing-and-retainage-the-slow-leak-in-construction-cash-flow/)). Rabbet is a vendor.
- 43% of subs lack working capital for surprises ([Billd 2025, same source](https://www.finoya.ai/blog/progress-billing-and-retainage-the-slow-leak-in-construction-cash-flow/)).
- Retainage releases require closeout documents: lien waivers, warranties, as-builts, O&M manuals, final CPRs, consent of surety ([SW Recovery](https://www.swrecovery.com/industries/construction-collections/)).

**Who pays today.**
- Notices: Levelset (its pricing page now shows **$59 per recipient** for prelims and demand notices, with job research included ([Levelset pricing](https://www.levelset.com/pricing/)); older listings said $19; lien $349); SunRay ($25–$35); Notice Zone ($55); NACM ($30 + $260/yr); NCS (tiered).
- Collections: agencies at 10–25% contingency.
- Billing and lien waivers: Siteline (custom annual) and LienWaiver.pro ($49/mo).
- AR outsourcing: from $350/mo ([Steph's Books](https://stephsbooks.com/our-services/accounts-receivable-services)).

**Why it's weak.**
- The barrier to sending prelims is **behavioral** (fear of the GC), not tooling. Levelset already makes it nearly free.
- Retainage collection by a third party is collection activity, and **commercial collection-agency licensing varies by state** ([Cornerstone](https://cornerstonelicensing.com/resources/first-party-vs-third-party-collections/); [Illinois 2026 change](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/illinois-revamps-the-scope-of-its-collection-agency-licensing-law)).
- Stay compliant by keeping to *pre-delinquency* reminders in the client's name and closeout document assembly. Leave contingency collection to licensed agencies.
- **New since July 2025:** CA SB 1286 extends the Rosenthal Act's conduct rules to commercial debts of $500k or less, including those of creditors and their agents ([Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/california-expands-scope-states-fair-debt-collection-practices-act-certain)). It adds no licensing requirement.
- Missed competitors: CRF Solutions (prelims, liens and collections) and construction AR outsourcers such as VJM Global ([VJM](https://www.vjmglobal.com/services/outsource-accounts-receivable-construction-industry)).

**Pricing.** $299/mo plus pass-through postage and notice fees.

**Automation:** about 80%. Humans handle GC relationship calls and lien filing decisions (with attorney referral).

**Reply rate:** 3–5%.

**Kill risks:** Levelset/Procore Pay bundles, behavioral inertia, licensing.

**Validation:**
- Email 500 CA/TX/FL subs found from public permit/PWC-100 data.
- **Kill if** fewer than 3 paid at $299 by day 30.

**Validation.**
- Offer it to the existing customer base with a 1-page landing page and a $199/mo founding price.
- **Kill if** the attach rate is below 3% of active trade customers within 30 days, or average human time exceeds 2 hrs/client/month.
