## A. AI + Maps visibility / reviews retainer, as an upsell to existing website customers

**Pitch.** Sell a done-for-you "Reviews + GBP + Citations" plan to the brothers' own site customers, with AI visibility shown in the monthly report as the hook rather than sold as the product (verify-5).

**Problem / who pays today**
- SMBs already pay for reviews: Podium $399–$599/mo ([socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/reviews/blogs/podium-pricing)), Birdeye about $299–449/location ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/review-management/birdeye/)), NiceJob $75/$125 ([NiceJob](https://get.nicejob.com/pricing)).
- 45% of consumers have used AI for local recommendations, but ChatGPT named only about 1.2% of 350K local locations, against 35.9% for Google's 3-pack ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/lcrs-ai-trust/), [MarketingCode](https://www.marketingcode.com/chatgpt-recommends-1-percent-local-businesses-contractors/)). Most clients will see "0% visibility".
- SMB willingness to pay for AI-visibility *monitoring* specifically is still unproven (verify-5).

**Data / inputs & access**
- Places API: $17–$32 per 1K calls ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)). The review gap is deterministic, so use it as the cold-outreach proof.
- **Verifier correction:** answers from the LLM API overlap only **15.5–23.8%** with what the consumer UI shows ([Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/llm-scraped-ai-answers-vs-api-results)). Fewer than 1 in 100 runs return the same brand list ([SEL](https://searchengineland.com/ai-recommendation-lists-rarely-repeat-study-468076)). Report a mention rate across at least 10 samples per prompt, never a "rank".
- GBP API: no lead generation; edits need consent ([policies](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/policies)).

**Buyer + market size**
- The buyer is the brothers' existing website customers. Their count is **not established**.
- Lane-wide universe: about 2.0M employer establishments in the target verticals (lane 09, CBP 2023).

**Pricing & recurring model.** $79–$129/mo, month to month (verify-5). This replaces the lane's $149.

**Agent pipeline**
- **Find:** tag existing customers by vertical.
- **Build deliverable:** review gap against the map pack, plus mention rate (cost about $0.30–0.75 per snapshot).
- **Personalized outreach:** in-account email.
- **Handle replies:** agent answers and sends a checkout link.
- **Deliver/renew:** GBP posts, review requests, citations, monthly report.
- **Automatable:** about 90% for snapshots and reports, 80% overall (verify-5).
- **Human touchpoints:** GBP suspensions and reinstatements, onboarding the review feed, "my phone shows something different" disputes.

**First 90 days.** Offer the plan to every existing site customer. Build it on wholesale tools instead of a custom sampler. Keep the 60-day take-rate gate below.

**Unit economics**
- CAC is about $0 for existing customers.
- Cost: wholesale BrightLocal $31 or Merchynt about $20/profile, plus the lane's estimated $15–25/mo COGS.
- Churn: SMB SaaS runs 3–7%/mo. Yext customers under $50K ARR keep 69% of dollars per year ([Yext](https://investors.yext.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/391/yext-announces-second-quarter-fiscal-2027-results)).
- Cold outbound, if attempted: model 0.2–0.5% close, which gives a CAC of about $150–300.

**Competitors (with prices)**
- BrightLocal AI tracker from **$31/mo**, included as standard ([official](https://www.brightlocal.com/local-seo-tools/local-ai-visibility/)).
- Local Falcon $24.99–$199.99.
- Merchynt Paige $99/location.
- Profound $99/$399.
- Peec €85–€425.
- Localo $39–49.
- LLMrefs $79.
- Rankscale €20.

**Legal / licensing**
- No licence needed.
- FTC substantiation: word results as "in N of M sampled API answers" ([accessiBe order](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-order-requires-online-marketer-pay-1-million-deceptive-claims-its-ai-product-could-make-websites)).
- FTC fake-review rule, up to $51,744 per violation ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Google bans review gating.
- CAN-SPAM applies. Contact owners by email only, because texting brings in the TCPA.

**Top risks**
- A client checks ChatGPT on their own phone, sees a different answer and cancels.
- An agency gives away the same report inside a $31 BrightLocal seat.
- The lane's "12–18-month window" claim is wrong: **the window has already closed**.

**Cheapest validation test + kill criteria**
- Offer the plan to all existing site customers.
- **Kill if** fewer than 10% take it within 60 days (verify-5).

**Verification status.** WEAKENED. **5.0** as an upsell, 3.5 as cold outbound, medium-high confidence (verify-5). The CAC is near zero and the work is real done-for-you work, but the AI report alone is a $31 commodity.

**Final check (verify-6):** CONFIRMED at **5.0**. This is the best single upsell. It works best when built into the acquisition offer (site plus Local Presence Plan from day one).

## B. Manufacturer co-op advertising claim desk, as an upsell

**Pitch.** Pay for dealers' and contractors' sites and ads partly out of manufacturer co-op funds: the agents handle pre-approvals and claims, and the co-op covers 40–70% of the brothers' retainer.

**Problem / who pays today**
- Carrier's 2026 policy pays 50% against a 1.5–2% accrual. Websites need pre-approval, claims are due within 60 days, and there is a hard 15 Dec cutoff ([Carrier Enterprise](https://resource.carrierenterprise.com/is/content/Watscocom/ce_ca_carrier-program-benefits_pdf_20260304_2026_Co-Op_AdvertisingPolicy_Carrier-Janpdf)).
- Yamaha pays 70% instead of 40% only when the dealer uses an approved agency ([MPN](https://www.motorcyclepowersportsnews.com/breakdown-oem-co-op-changes-2026/)).
- **Verifier correction:** "$14–35B unclaimed" is a 2016 trade-group estimate and should be treated as marketing. Retroactive recovery is "mostly fiction". Only unspent current-period accrual can be recovered.

**Data / inputs & access**
- Program guides are often public PDFs.
- Accrual balances need dealer-portal access; use delegated seats rather than shared logins.
- Proof of performance comes from the brothers' own ad and site logs.

**Buyer + market size**
- Owners of HVAC contractors and of powersports, marine, outdoor power equipment (OPE) and appliance dealers carrying national brands.
- About 84k brand-affiliated HVAC/plumbing contractors (unverified), plus 15–20k non-auto dealers.
- Unclaimed co-op per dealer is about $3k–$20k/yr (est.).
- The serviceable base is the brothers' existing customers, whose count is **not established**.

**Pricing & recurring model.** Either 15–20% of recovered co-op or, preferably, a monthly retainer of $500–$1,500 with co-op funding 40–70% of it. Both are recurring.

**Agent pipeline**
- **Find:** match dealer locators against the brands each dealer carries (95% automatable).
- **Build deliverable:** a digest of the program rules plus an estimated balance (70%).
- **Personalized outreach:** 90%.
- **Handle replies:** 80%.
- **Deliver/renew:** pre-approvals and claim packets (70%); renewal (90%).
- **Human touchpoints:** portal logins, OEM pre-approval nuances, applications for approved-agency status.

**First 90 days**
- Pick HVAC, where brand mix is visible on websites.
- Pitch existing site customers first.
- Then cold-email with a brand-specific estimate.
- Apply for OEM approved-agency status (Yamaha, Honda).

**Unit economics.** A $900/mo retainer with about $450 funded by co-op (est.). Agent cost is about $40/mo and human time about 1 hr/mo. Expected churn is lower when co-op covers part of the bill.

**Competitors (with prices)**
- CoopReclaim: $99/mo is planned; it is currently in free early access ([site](https://coopreclaim.com/guide.html)).
- Dealer Spike: about $495–$2,000+/mo.
- Ansira/BrandMuscle: from about $4,500/mo, sold to OEMs.
- RevLocal, MPW and TM Rally.
- In auto, Strong Automotive bundles co-op free with full service.

**Legal / licensing.** No licence is needed. Program terms are the constraint: pre-approval, approved-agency lists and trademark rules.

**Top risks**
- OEM approved-agency lock-in: Yamaha, Honda, Kawasaki and Lennox's Preferred Vendor Program.
- Portal-access friction.
- Incumbents bundle claims for free.

**Cheapest validation test + kill criteria**
- Audit the brand programs of 20 existing customers that carry national brands.
- **Kill if** fewer than 5 have at least $2k/yr of unspent accrual, or fewer than 2 convert to a retainer.

**Verification status.** WEAKENED. **4.5** overall: **5.0** as an upsell, about 3 as standalone cold outbound, medium confidence (lane 13 self-verification). It works as a close-rate and retention add-on, not as a found-money business.

**Final check (verify-6):** WEAKENED. **4.0** overall, rising to 5.0 only where 10% or more of the base are national-brand dealers. A standalone claim desk is effectively KILLED (2.5). The co-op-funded marketing retainer for dealers survives.

## C. SMB technology-advisor residual book + bill audit, as an upsell

**Pitch.** Agents audit existing customers' internet, phone, POS and processing bills and switch them where that saves money. The vendors then pay a residual for the life of the contract.

**Problem / who pays today**
- Carriers and SaaS vendors pay advisors **10–22% of monthly recurring charges (MRC)** for the life of the contract ([telecom.directory](https://www.telecom.directory/resources/agent-commissions)).
- Merchant agents earn 50–70% of the processing markup ([Unison](https://www.unisonpayment.com/blog/merchant-services-agent-program-residual-income)).
- The technology services distributor (TSD) channel billed **$16.6B in 2024** ([Omdia](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/blogs/2026/jan/key-insights-from-the-16point6bn-dollars-technology-services-distribution-tsd-market)).
- **Verifier correction:** Comcast and Spectrum pay *one-time* bounties (up to $1,500 or $5,000), not residuals ([Comcast](https://business.comcast.com/partner/authorized-connector-program)).

**Data / inputs & access.** The customer uploads bills, which agents OCR. Payouts flow through TSD or ISO agreements.

**Buyer + market size**
- The buyer is the brothers' existing site customers. Their count is **not established**.
- Lane example: 200 customers × 30% conversion × $75 ≈ $4.5k/mo (est.).

**Pricing & recurring model**
- Free to the customer; the vendor pays.
- About **$60–100/mo per fully switched customer**, starting 60–150 days after signing (est.).
- Optional second line: a savings-share fee. Comparables are BillCutterz 50%, Billshark 40%, Trim 33% and Dip $15/mo ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/select/best-bill-negotiation-services/)).

**Agent pipeline**
- **Find:** existing customers.
- **Build deliverable:** a "bill audit" comparison from OCR (85% automatable).
- **Personalized outreach:** to own customers (90%).
- **Handle replies:** agent.
- **Deliver/renew:** order provisioning and porting through carrier portals (40%).
- **Overall:** about 65%. Humans handle provisioning and porting.

**First 90 days.** Offer a free bill audit to 100 existing customers and switch those who save. The 60-day gate is below.

**Unit economics**
- Example residuals: internet at $150 MRC × 15% = $22; 5-seat UCaaS = $30; processing = $45.
- Payment risks: chargeback windows of about 6 months, and some ISOs vest residuals only after 12–24 months.

**Competitors (with prices)**
- Telarus, Intelisys, Avant, AppDirect, Sandler and BridgePointe pay advisors about 80% of supplier commission; the top six hold 72.3% of the channel.
- Lightyear ($31M raised) is free to the buyer.
- Merchant ISOs pay 50–70% splits.
- MSPs bundle connectivity.

**Legal / licensing**
- No licence is needed for telecom or SaaS agency work.
- Merchant services needs a sponsor-bank or ISO agreement.
- Exclude energy, which requires state registration in TX, PA, IL, OH, NY and others.
- Disclose vendor compensation to the customer.

**Top risks**
- Small dollars per account.
- Carrier-dependent payouts.
- Chargebacks on early churn.
- A 2–5 month lag before the first check.

**Cheapest validation test + kill criteria**
- Run the free bill audit with 100 existing customers.
- **Kill if** fewer than 15 upload bills, or fewer than 5 switch within 60 days.
- Expected reply rate: 15–30% from existing customers (est.), against 1–3% cold.

**Verification status.** Holds at **5.0** as an upsell, 3.0 cold, medium confidence (lane 15 Verifier B). It is the only recurring, unlicensed, warm-channel model in the lane, but the dollars are modest.

**Final check (verify-6):** WEAKENED to **3.5** for telecom and merchant referral only. **Energy brokerage is KILLED (1.5)** without per-state licensing. Treat it as an opportunistic referral line, not a product.

## D. Contractor back-office bundle (prevailing wage / prequal / lien notices), as an upsell

**Pitch.** "Your website's live. For $199/mo we also handle your paperwork deadlines": licence and insurance tracking, COIs, sub W-9s and COIs, WC-audit document collection, 1099s, prelims, and certified payroll when the customer wins a public job.

**Problem / who pays today**

**Data / inputs & access**
- The customer's own documents and payroll exports.
- The DIR eCPR portal allows linked users, but the contractor signs each upload ([PWCR guide](https://www.dir.ca.gov/public-works/PWCR-Guide-External-6.2023.pdf)).
- ISN allows independent-contractor users ([ISN](https://www.isnetworld.com/en/user-agreements/contractor-supplier)).

**Buyer + market size**
- The buyer is the brothers' existing trade customers.
- If N=500 and 10–15% attach at $199, that is about $120–180k ARR (est.).
- The true N and the share doing commercial or public work are **not established**.

**Pricing & recurring model.** $199/mo founding price. Add prevailing-wage and prequal modules only if attach is at least 3%.

**Agent pipeline**
- **Find:** tag customers by licence class and by website keywords ("commercial", "public works", "ISNetworld").
- **Build deliverable:** a 1-page offer.
- **Personalized outreach:** in-product.
- **Handle replies:** agent.
- **Deliver/renew:** calendar-driven filings.
- **Automatable:** about 70%.
- **Human touchpoints:** the owner signs eCPR certifications; classification judgment calls; safety-professional review of prequal programs; edge cases.

**First 90 days**
1. Count the commercial and public-works share of the customer base. If it is under 20%, drop the idea.
2. Run a 30-day attach test.
3. Build modules only if attach is at least 3%.

**Unit economics**
- CAC is about $0 because the buyers are existing customers.
- The kill line is human time above 2 hrs/client/month.
- Bundle gross margin is **not established**. For comparison, the standalone payroll desk ran about 75% at 1.5 hrs of review per month.

**Competitors (with prices)**
- Wellstanding certified-payroll desk: **$995 setup + $249/mo** ([site](https://wellstanding.com/)).
- EHS Inc Bronze: $1,950/yr (about $163/mo).
- Avetta's own done-for-you "Vetify" tier.
- Levelset: $59 per recipient.
- FL notice-to-owner (NTO) services: $25–$65.
- CertifiedPayrollPro: $49–$99/mo + $3/report.
- LCPcertified: $12/report.
- TrackMyVendor: $39/mo.
- QuickBooks Online Premium/Elite now produces WH-347.

**Legal / licensing**
- No class-code review or insurance advice: FL FS 624.10(5)(d) and 626.015 ([statute](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/624.10)).
- No collections: CA SB 1286 extends Rosenthal conduct rules to commercial debt of $500k or less.
- Never touch payroll funds.
- Carry E&O, at an unverified ~$10/client/mo.

**Top risks**
- Customers may skew toward consumer-facing micro trades, which shrinks the value to reminders worth about $20/mo.
- Scope creep turns it into a low-margin VA business.
- ISN may terminate users who are affiliated with other subscribers.

**Cheapest validation test + kill criteria**
- 30-day offer with a landing page, at about $0 cost.
- **Kill if** attach is under 3% of active trade customers, or human time exceeds 2 hrs per client per month.

**Verification status.** **4.5**, low confidence (lane 12 self-verification). The standalone modules were cut to 3.5–4.0 because Wellstanding and Avetta Vetify already fill the done-for-you gap, so only the bundle sold to the existing base survives.

**Final check (verify-6):** WEAKENED to **3.0** for typical residential-trades customers, and 4.0 only for a commercial-subcontractor segment.

## F. Shipping-data competitor reports (seed idea b), best surviving variant: broker white-label tariff/sourcing alert engine

**Pitch.** Sell customs brokers a white-label client-alert engine. It reports *exposure categories*, not dollar figures ("you import HS 9403 from Vietnam; the §301 action applies at 12.5%; ask your broker"), and uses the Sourcing-Shift report as a free hook.

**Problem / who pays today**
- Tariff churn is extreme: IEEPA was struck down, then came §122, then §301 at 10–12.5% on 60 economies ([GTA](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/forced-labour-section-301-final-action)).
- Small importers pay $2,500–$7,500 per tariff assessment ([Importivity](https://importivity.com/blog/tariff-consulting-turns-customs-complexity/)).
- **Verifier correction:** I found no evidence that SMBs pay $199–499/mo for monitoring based on bills of lading (BOL). Brokers and Flexport give alerts away free.

**Data / inputs & access**
- The CBP AMS feed is priced at "production cost", with **no published price**, and a 2023 FOIA request for it was refused ([Data Liberation Project](https://www.data-liberation-project.org/requests/cbp-bills-of-lading/)).
- Manifests carry no value and no HTS code. Values are missing on 64.6–68.3% of records, and consignees on **8.0–35.1%** ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2021066pap.pdf)).
- Ocean freight is about 50% of import value.
- ImportYeti's terms allow only personal, internal use ([ToS](https://www.importyeti.com/policies/terms)).

**Buyer + market size**
- About 11,000–11,400 broker licences and about 2,500 brokerage firms ([customsbrokerindex](https://customsbrokerindex.com/blog/customs-broker-list-how-to-find/)), plus 9,078 FMC-licensed ocean transportation intermediaries (OTIs).
- Penetration and revenue are **not established**.

**Pricing & recurring model.** A flat monthly subscription only, never priced per lead (see Legal). The price is **not established**. Anchors: ImportGenius $229/$449/mo; the forwarder trigger idea at $300–1,000/mo.

**Agent pipeline**
- **Find:** brokers via the licence and FMC lists.
- **Build deliverable:** sample alerts on the broker's own clients' lanes.
- **Personalized outreach:** to the broker.
- **Handle replies:** agent demo.
- **Deliver/renew:** cron re-runs on each Federal Register or CSMS tariff notice.
- **Automatable:** about 85% (lane).
- **Human touchpoints:** HS-imputation QA, an annual rate-logic review by a licensed broker, sales calls.

**First 90 days**
1. Get a CBP feed quote by phone **before writing code**.
2. Hand-build 50 briefs and have 2 licensed brokers check them.
3. Send 500.

**Unit economics.** The lane's numbers are importer-side: LLM under $0.20 per brief, data $500–3,000/mo. Broker-side CAC and churn are **not established**.

**Competitors (with prices)**
- Descartes Datamyne Tariff Insights (USA Import $6,995/yr).
- Altana.
- ImportGenius lead-gen tier.
- Flexport free simulator.
- Gaia: $0–$1,399/mo, about 800 accounts.
- Tarifflo.
- ImportYeti: free; Enterprise $1,000/mo.

**Legal / licensing**
- An estimate is outside "customs business". Protests, CAPE filings and classifications for others are inside it ([19 USC 1641](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1641)).
- Per-lead or per-client fees from brokers are grey to prohibited ([H276784](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/H276784)). Use a flat fee only, and consider a free binding ruling under 19 CFR 177.

**Top risks**
- Any importer told "we can see your shipments" can mask itself in about 24 hours.
- The tariffs themselves are being litigated.
- There is no data moat.
- The sourcing report usually says "nothing changed this month".

**Cheapest validation test + kill criteria**
- Get the CBP phone quote first; then 50 broker-checked briefs and 500 sends.
- **Kill if** trial conversion is below 1% (lane threshold), or the brokers judge the briefs inaccurate.

**Verification status.** WEAKENED. The seed Sourcing-Shift report scores **4.0** (verify-5) and is useful only as a hook. The tariff brief scores **3.5** when re-scoped to brokers (verify-1). Alerts are a commodity, masking runs up to 35%, and the feed price is unknown.

## G. California stormwater citizen-suit risk monitor

**Pitch.** Replicate the plaintiffs' screen (exceedances, unsampled rain days) on public data, then sell a fixed-fee "pre-notice audit" and monitoring through QISP firms and defense counsel.

**Problem / who pays today**

## H. Pro-se trademark office-action workbench for attorneys

**Pitch.** An attorney-side workbench that decodes each office action (OA), drafts a response skeleton, quote and engagement letter, and optionally sends exclusive-territory postal outreach to unrepresented applicants.

**Problem / who pays today**
- Attorneys charge $500–$3,500 per OA response, but the floor is **$89** (L4SB) and Trademark Engine charges $599 ([L4SB](https://www.l4sb.com/services/trademark-office-action-response/)).
- Small firms pay $60–$295/mo for trademark tooling ([Alt Legal](https://www.altlegal.com/pricing/)).

**Data / inputs & access**
- Pro-se emails are public on TSDR: all 42 pro-se records in a 200-record sample had one.
- **Verifier correction:** the daily XML has **no email element**, so email needs one TSDR call per serial.
- Rate limits: 60/min (status) and 4/min (documents), or 120 and 12 per minute off-peak ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/tm-enterprise-api-user-guide-v2.pdf)).

**Buyer + market size**
- 50–150 mid-volume solo attorneys.
- More than 824,000 application classes in FY2025. Pro se is about 30% (28.5% per USPTO FY17; 26% per [Gerhardt & Lee](https://www.inta.org/wp-content/uploads/public-files/resources/the-trademark-reporter/TMR-Vol-112-No-06_Gerhardt-Lee.pdf)).
- An estimated 90–130k pro-se OAs a year.
- Attorneys with 100+ filings (5% of attorneys) handle **79%** of filings. The lane's 3,000–6,000 small-firm buyers is unverifiable.

**Pricing & recurring model**
- Flat subscription, never a percentage of fees (Rule 5.4).
- Lane pricing: $299 or $799/mo, or $15–40 per lead.
- Verifier: at a ~$600 fee a lead is worth $6–12 to the attorney, so even $7.50 is barely break-even.

**Agent pipeline**
- **Find:** rank attorneys by XML filings.
- **Build deliverable:** 5 decoded sample OAs.
- **Personalized outreach:** email to attorneys.
- **Handle replies:** agent plus a human for the first deals.
- **Deliver/renew:** nightly ingest, drafts, and a retained-matter report built from TSDR appearances.
- **Automatable:** about 85%.
- **Human touchpoints:** the first sales calls, per-state template compliance review, classifier QA.

**First 90 days**
- Back-test on six months of OAs.
- Filter out filing-mill correspondents and non-US "pro se" records.
- Ship TX/FL compliance packs.
- Run a 60-day pilot with 3 firms.

**Unit economics.** Lane estimates: blended ARPA about $500/mo, compute $0.05–0.15 per OA, gross margin over 90%, CAC $300–800.

**Competitors (with prices)**
- About 30 Apify USPTO actors, from **$2.79 per 1k** records; effective crowding about 7/10.
- LegalZoom, Trademark Engine and Trademarkia upsell their own pro-se customers.

**Legal / licensing**
- Only attorneys represent applicants (37 CFR 11.14).
- 11.703 bans live contact only.
- TX: "ADVERTISEMENT" in the subject line and filing within 10 days. FL: filing at least 20 days ahead, $150 per ad.
- Under ABA Op. 501, the subscribing lawyer answers for the vendor's contact methods.

**Top risks**
- The channel is poisoned by scams: USPTO-impersonation and the 2026 "Mandatory Verification Appointment" scheme.
- **Correction:** 46% of pro-se applicants *succeed*, so about 54% fail.
- A 3-month extension for $125 softens the deadline hook.

**Cheapest validation test + kill criteria**
- 60-day pilot with 3 firms.
- **Kill if** retained matters come in under about 1% of contacts.

**Verification status.** WEAKENED. **4.5**, medium confidence (verify-4). The data is real, but raw records cost about $3 per 1k, the channel is scam-poisoned, and the buyer base is concentrated.

**Final check (verify-7):** WORSE, **3.5**. The USPTO scam page now names "letters offering to represent you after an Office Action" as a scam pattern. The workbench pivot is crowded (ActionResponder, Questel Qthena). Deprioritised.

## I. RV / heavy-truck dealer warranty retail-rate uplift (RateLift)

**Pitch.** File the statutory retail-rate warranty submission (100 sequential repair orders) for RV and heavy-truck dealers in states whose franchise act covers them, then re-file every year.

**Problem / who pays today**
- Auto dealers pay en masse. Armatus has 7,400+ dealers, with average gains of $97,488 (parts) plus $73,116 (labor) per store per year ([Armatus](https://www.dealeruplift.com/about-us/)).

**Data / inputs & access**
- Public statutes: FL 320.696 ties RV warranty pay to retail charges ([FL](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/320.696)). SD amended its truck statute in 2021 to require retail rates.
- Dealer lists come from OEM locators.
- Repair-order exports need the dealer's cooperation, which is the main friction.

**Buyer + market size**
- About 2,980 RV dealerships ([VerticalIQ](https://verticaliq.com/product/rv-dealers/)). The heavy-truck count is **not established**.
- Uplift per RV or truck store is **not established**.
- **Verifier correction:** the equipment, OPE, marine and powersports segments are cut. Their statutes mostly require only a rate notice, parts are fixed at net +15%, and per-store uplift is about $3–10k.

**Pricing & recurring model.** 30% of the first 12 months of uplift, or $2,500–5,000 flat. Recurring revenue comes from annual re-filing and from monitoring OEM credits.

**Agent pipeline**
- **Find:** 95% automatable.
- **Build deliverable:** estimate from posted labor rates (70%).
- **Personalized outreach:** 90%.
- **Handle replies:** 80%.
- **Deliver:** 60%; renew 85%.
- **Overall:** about 75%.
- **Human touchpoints:** sales call, obtaining the repair-order export, dealer signature, OEM objections (dealer counsel).

**First 90 days**
1. 10-state RV statute check, starting with FL 320.696.
2. 200 personalised emails.
3. Test whether an association endorsement is reachable.

**Unit economics**
- Lane estimates, not RV-specific: about $6k first-year fee and $1.5k renewal per dealer; CAC $500–1,500; about $300 compute plus 3 hours of review; about 80% gross margin.
- Cash arrives 2–6 months after filing.
- Manufacturing reply benchmarks run about 2–4% (verify-1).

**Competitors (with prices)**
- Armatus: contingent, percentage undisclosed.
- Withum: $7,500.
- Wooden/Warranty Part: pricing contradictory.
- Bellavia Cohen and QB Business Solutions.
- Aclaro and Chameleon WarrantyAi: software.
- **Note:** for RV and truck, the 100-RO mechanism exists only under the auto franchise acts, which is exactly where these firms already operate.

**Legal / licensing**
- No licence was found for the submission itself.
- Demand letters and motor-vehicle-board protests go to dealer counsel.
- *AEM v. Burgum* (2019) limits new statutes in the 8th Circuit.

**Top risks**
- Incumbent auto filers extend into RV and truck.
- Dealers refuse to send repair-order exports to an unknown vendor.
- OEMs audit or charge back.

**Cheapest validation test + kill criteria**
- The 10-state check plus 200 emails.

**Verification status.** WEAKENED. **4.0** for RV/truck (3.0 for equipment and marine), low-to-medium confidence (lane 13 self-verification). The per-store pools outside auto are small, and RV/truck sits inside the incumbents' auto-act turf.

**Final check (verify-7):** **KILLED for RV, WEAKENED for truck: 2.0.** RV statutes guarantee only the lowest reasonable retail rate plus a fixed parts markup, with no 100-RO mechanism, and truck OEMs already run their own labour-rate process.
