## 1. Lane overview

**The thesis.** The client pays only out of money the agents recover or save. That makes the sale easy ("no recovery, no fee"), and the work is mostly reading statements, invoices and contracts and filing paperwork, which agents can now do.

**The pattern behind the survivors.** The ideas that hold up share three features. They sell to a **small, identifiable B2B list** (dealers and manufacturers, not consumers or guarded professionals). Their recovery right is **statutory and recurring**, not a one-off error. And no lawyer, CPA, customs broker, public adjuster or insurance licensee is needed. That combination is rare.

## 2. Candidate ideas

The ideas are ordered by my pre-verification score. Section 4 records the adversarial verification; Section 5 gives the final ranking. Some draft claims are struck or corrected inline.

### Idea 1: "RateLift": retail-rate warranty reimbursement submissions for **non-auto** dealers (farm, outdoor-power and construction equipment; powersports and motorcycle; marine; RV; heavy truck)

**Pitch.** Almost every state has a dealer franchise statute that requires manufacturers to pay dealers their *retail* labor rate and parts markup for warranty work. But the law is not self-executing: the dealer must file a submission, typically 100 sequential customer-pay repair orders ([Armatus playbook](https://www.dealeruplift.com/retail-warranty-reimbursement-article/), [IADA dealer guide](https://votervoice.s3.amazonaws.com/groups/ilada/attachments/Dealer%20Guide%20to%20Warranty%20Reimbursement.pdf)). Car dealers are heavily served. Equipment, powersports, marine and RV dealers have similar statutes in many states, but far fewer specialised filers. The agents would:
1. ingest the dealer's repair-order export;
2. compute the effective labor rate and parts markup under that state's formula;
3. draft the statutory submission for the dealer to sign;
4. track the OEM's 30–60-day response window and any objection;
5. re-file annually where the statute allows.

**Data and inputs.**
- *Statutes:* public (state code sites, e.g. [MN 325E.0631](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/325e.0631), [Missouri 407.857 OPE labor rate](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/missouri/mo-laws/missouri_laws_407-857), [CT ch. 743r farm/forestry/OPE](https://cga.ct.gov/2022/sup/chap_743r.htm), [Kansas 16-120](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch16/016_001_0020.html), [EDA statute handout](https://farmequip.org/media/McGrath-State%20Statutes%20Handout.pdf), [NMMA marine warranty laws](https://www.nmma.net/assets/cabinets/Cabinet446/State%20Level%20Warranty%20Laws%20for%20Boat%20and%20Accessory%20Mfgs.pdf)).
- *Dealer list:* OEM dealer locators and association member lists (public).
- *Repair orders:* from the dealer's DMS export (Lightspeed, IntelliDealer, e-Emphasys and similar). This needs the dealer's cooperation, and that cooperation is the main friction.

**Buyer.** Dealer principal or service manager at independent equipment, powersports, marine, RV and truck dealers. Dealer-principals are owner-operators and are not "guarded professionals".

**Evidence that money moves today.**
- Armatus Dealer Uplift (auto) now lists 7,400+ dealers, 19,000+ approvals and 25 association endorsements, with average gains of $97,488 (parts) plus $73,116 (labor) per store per year, on a fully contingent fee. (The draft's earlier "$101,695" figure could not be re-verified.) ([Armatus about](https://www.dealeruplift.com/about-us/), [FAQ](https://www.dealeruplift.com/faq/)).
- Withum charges a flat $7,500 per submission ([Withum](https://www.withum.com/industries/dealerships/warranty-reimbursement/)).
- Bellavia Cohen charges a flat fee and also serves marine dealers ([dealerlaw.com](https://www.dealerlaw.com/retail-warranty-reimbursement/)).
- QB Business Solutions (auto, contingent) claims $280k–$500k per year uplifts ([QB](https://qbbusinesssolutions.com/warranty-reimbursement/)).
- Chameleon "WarrantyAi" reports average labor-rate increases of $30.98 ([Chameleon](https://www.chameleonlimited.com/solutions/warrantyai)).
- In equipment, OEMs litigate over these statutes: Deere, CNH, AGCO and Kubota sued over North Dakota's SB 2289. In **2019** the 8th Circuit affirmed an injunction barring its application to *pre-existing* dealer contracts (*AEM v. *Uplift per store (est.):*
- Ag store: about 1,000 warranty labor hours × $25/hr ≈ $25k, plus parts-markup uplift of about $20k, so about $45k/yr.
- Powersports or marine store: about $8k–$20k/yr.

*Revenue pool:* with 25% of rooftops eligible and not yet filed, and a fee equal to 3–4 months of uplift (about $5k–$12k), the first-time pool is roughly **$20M–$45M**, plus annual re-filings. That is a real niche but small.

**Deliverable and pricing.** A filed statutory submission plus the OEM correspondence. The fee is either 30% of the first 12 months of uplift, billed monthly from the dealer's warranty credit statements, or a flat $2,500–$5,000. Recurring revenue comes from:
- annual re-submissions, which most statutes allow once every 12 months;
- monitoring OEM compliance, i.e. catching claims paid at the old rate.

**Automation pipeline.**

| Stage | Automatable | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Find | 95% | Scrape OEM dealer locators; tag each dealer with the state statute that applies to its *product category* (this varies) |
| Build | 70% | Model a pre-pitch estimate from public posted labor rates (many dealers post them on their sites), then the exact computation once the RO export arrives |
| Outreach | 90% | Per-dealer email: "Your state's [statute] lets you bill [OEM] at your $X/hr retail rate. You're likely paid ~$Y." |
| Replies | 80% | Agent answers FAQs and books a call |
| Deliver | 60% | RO export (human: dealer's office manager); agent computes the rate and drafts; **human reviews the first submissions per OEM** |
| Renew | 85% | Calendar re-filing; monitor credit memos |

Overall about 75% automatable. The human touchpoints that remain:
- a short sales call for most deals;
- getting the RO export;
- the dealer signing the submission;
- handling OEM audits and objections, which sometimes needs a dealer attorney.

**90-day GTM.**
- *Weeks 1–3:* build a 50-state matrix of which product categories carry a retail-rate requirement. Pick 3–4 states with clean statutes (e.g. MN, MO, CT and one marine-statute state).
- *Weeks 3–6:* send 1,500 personalised emails to equipment, powersports and marine dealers in those states.
- *Weeks 6–12:* aim for 10 signed dealers and 3 filed submissions, and test whether an association endorsement is reachable (Armatus's channel was 24 association endorsements).

**Unit economics (est.).**
- Per signed dealer: about $6k of first-year fee, $1.5k renewal.
- CAC: about $500–$1,500 (cold email plus a call).
- Delivery cost: about $300 of agent compute and about 3 hours of human review.
- Gross margin: about 80%.
- Cash lags filing by 2–6 months: 30–60 days of OEM approval, then monthly warranty credits.

Net: auto is crowded; non-auto is *thinner but not empty*.

**Why can't the buyer do this themselves, or buy it for $50/mo?** A dealer can file alone. Statutes deliberately make it possible, and associations publish guides. But the computation rules differ by state and by product category, OEMs reject sloppy submissions, and service managers don't do it. Auto dealers, who are better staffed, still pay contingent firms en masse. No $50/mo self-serve tool for non-auto dealers was found.

**Is the trigger early enough?** Yes. There is no "trigger" that arrives too late: the right is ongoing and re-arms every 12 months. A secondary trigger is a newly enacted state statute (e.g. NC S.L. 2025-41 for autos, [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/driving-forward-developments-in-transportation-law-and-innovation/all/reasonableness-in-state-warranty-reimbursement-statutes-takes-a-hit-in-2025)).

**Licensing and fee-splitting.** No licence was found for preparing a dealer's commercial submission, and the incumbents (Armatus, Warranty Part) are not law firms. Risks:
1. Drafting legal *demand letters* or handling an OEM protest before a state motor vehicle board is closer to law practice. Hand those to dealer counsel.
2. Contingent fees are commercial and unregulated here.
3. Some statutes restrict frequency, and some OEMs may audit or charge back over-claimed rates.

**Kill risks.**
- Non-auto statutes are patchier: many say "reasonable compensation", not retail rate. Uplift per store may be too small to sell.
- Wooden/Warranty Part or Armatus extends into equipment with association endorsements.
- Dealers refuse to export ROs to an unknown vendor.
- The 8th Circuit's ND ruling emboldens equipment OEMs to fight.

**Cheapest validation test (about $300, 3 weeks).**
1. Build the statute matrix for 10 states × 5 categories.
2. Email 300 ag/OPE, powersports and marine dealers in "clean" states with a computed estimate based on their posted labor rate.

*Kill criteria:* fewer than 4 positive replies, fewer than 2 dealers willing to send an RO export, or legal review finding fewer than 5 states with retail-rate (not "reasonable") language for the target category.

**Cold-email reply rate.** Manufacturing/industrial reply-rate benchmarks run about 2–6% ([revenueflow](https://www.revenueflow.com/blog/manufacturing-cold-email-benchmarks), [b2bdataindex](https://b2bdataindex.com/research/cold-email-benchmarks-2026/)). With a dealer-specific dollar figure I estimate **3–6% replies and 1–2% positive (est.)**.

### Idea 2: "Co-op Recovery + Co-op-Funded Marketing" for multi-brand local dealers and contractors (HVAC, powersports, marine, OPE, appliance)

**Pitch.** Manufacturers accrue co-op funds for dealers, often 2% of purchases, reimbursing 40–70% of eligible ad spend. A large share is never claimed: an estimated $14–35B per year, based on a 2016 LSA white paper ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/288514/up-to-35b-of-co-op-ad-funds-go-unclaimed-annually.html), [Mediagistic](https://www.mediagistic.com/blog/local-co-op-advertising-programs-why-are-billions-left-unspent-every-year)). The agents would:
1. read each dealer's brand programs;
2. find spend already incurred that is still claimable inside the 30–60-day post-period window;
3. file claims with proof of performance;
4. then run the brothers' **websites and ads as co-op-eligible spend**. Carrier's 2026 policy pays 50% against a 1.5–2% accrual and needs pre-approval for websites ([Carrier Enterprise 2026](https://resource.carrierenterprise.com/is/content/Watscocom/ce_ca_carrier-program-benefits_pdf_20260304_2026_Co-Op_AdvertisingPolicy_Carrier-Janpdf)). *(Correction: the draft attributed Trane, Lennox and Polaris figures to a 2017 ACCA post that does not contain them.)*

This turns "found money" into the funding source for the brothers' core product.

**Data and inputs.**
- Program guides: many are public PDFs ([Carrier guide](https://cacbdpapps.net/marketing/AdKits/cac2021/html/guide_coop.html)); some sit behind dealer portals.
- Accrual balances: dealer portal access needed.
- Proof of performance: the brothers' own ad and site logs, which they control.

**Buyer.** Owners of HVAC contractors and powersports, marine and OPE dealers. Many are already the brothers' website target.

**Evidence that money moves.**
- Auto co-op services: COOPABLE, Motivated Marketing, Co-Op Driver, Strong Automotive ([coopable](https://coopable.com/), [motivatedmarketing](https://motivatedmarketing.com/services/co-op-management/), [coopdriver](https://coopdriver.com/how-it-works/)).
- HVAC: RevLocal, MPW Marketing and All Contractor Marketing manage co-op claims as part of paid marketing ([RevLocal/ACCA](https://hvac-blog.acca.org/sponsored-content-let-revlocal-take-the-pain-out-of-your-hvac-co-op-program/), [MPW](https://myhvacmarketing.com/services/co-op-reimbursement/)).
- Powersports: Yamaha pays 70% instead of 40% only when the dealer uses one of three *approved agencies*, so OEM-sanctioned agency revenue exists ([MPN](https://www.motorcyclepowersportsnews.com/breakdown-oem-co-op-changes-2026/)).

**Market size (est.).**
- About 84k HVAC/plumbing contractors with brand affiliation **(unverified)**, plus ~15–20k non-auto dealers.
- Typical unclaimed co-op per dealer is perhaps $3k–$20k per year **(est.)**.
- The brothers' realistic capture is marketing retainers of $500–$1,500/mo, partly funded by co-op, so this is an *upsell multiplier* more than a standalone market.

**Deliverable and pricing.** Either:
- a "co-op claim desk" at 15–20% of recovered co-op (rare in the market; most agencies bundle it free with services ([Strong Automotive](https://strongautomotive.com/services/co-op/))); or
- the better model: a monthly marketing retainer where co-op covers 40–70% of the cost.

Both are recurring.

**Automation pipeline.**

| Stage | Automatable |
|---|---|
| Find (dealer locators × brands carried) | 95% |
| Build (program-rule digest and an estimated co-op balance from brand mix) | 70%; accruals need portal access |
| Outreach | 90% |
| Replies | 80% |
| Deliver (pre-approvals, claim packets, screenshots/invoices) | 70% |
| Renew | 90% |

Human touchpoints: portal logins (shared credentials raise policy issues; use delegated user seats where offered), OEM pre-approval nuances, and agency-approval applications.

**90-day GTM.**
1. Pick one vertical (HVAC: brand mix is visible on websites).
2. Pitch existing site customers first ("your new site may be 50–100% reimbursable by Trane/Lennox").
3. Then cold outbound with a brand-specific estimate.
4. Apply for OEM approved-agency status where programs have one (Yamaha, Honda SEM).

**Unit economics (est.).** A $900/mo retainer with about $450/mo funded by co-op improves close rate. Agent cost about $40/mo; human about 1 hr/mo. Churn is lower if co-op covers part of the bill.

**Competitors (8+ searches).**
- *Auto:* COOPABLE (first 30 days free, price n/a), Motivated Marketing, Co-Op Driver, Strong Automotive (free with full service).
- *HVAC:* RevLocal, MPW, All Contractor Marketing, TM Rally ([tmrally](https://tmrally.com/maximizing-hvac-co-op-funds-guide-to-helping-dealers/)).
- *Software:* **CoopReclaim at $99/mo for HVAC co-op claims** ([coopreclaim](https://coopreclaim.com/guide.html)); Co-op Connect (database, sold mainly to media companies, price on request) ([coopconnect](https://coopconnect.com/)); PowerChord PowerPartner (powersports/outdoor) ([PowerChord](https://www.powerchord.com/blog/dealer-co-op-funds)); Aptora (field-service software tracking co-op) ([Aptora](https://www.aptora.com/help/tips/co-op-manufacturer-rebates)).
- *OEM-approved agencies:* Yamaha (3), Honda, and Kawasaki (single AI-website vendor) ([MPN](https://www.motorcyclepowersportsnews.com/breakdown-oem-co-op-changes-2026/)).

Net: **crowded and OEM-gated**.

**Why can't they do it themselves, or buy it for $50/mo?** They can buy CoopReclaim at $99/mo. The real value is DFY marketing *plus* claims, which is the brothers' existing business.

**Trigger timing.** Program deadlines are hard. Claims are due 30–60 days after the period, e.g. CFMOTO funds expired 7 May 2026 with no rollover ([MPN](https://www.motorcyclepowersportsnews.com/breakdown-oem-co-op-changes-2026/)). Outreach must land *before* period-end. That is fine because the calendar is public.

**Licensing.** None. Program terms (pre-approval, approved agencies, trademark rules) are the constraint.

**Kill risks.** OEM approved-agency lock-in; portal-access friction; co-op covering less of the cost than claimed; incumbents bundling claims for free.

**Cheapest test.** Offer a free co-op audit to 20 existing site customers who carry national brands, then to 300 HVAC contractors by cold email. *Kill if* fewer than 5 accounts show at least $2k of claimable or unspent co-op, or fewer than 2 convert to a retainer.

**Cold-email reply rate.** For home-service contractors I estimate **2–5% (est.)**. Existing customers should be 20%+.

### Idea 3: Vendor-side sales-tax overcharge recovery for small manufacturers, contractors and multi-unit operators ("get it back from the vendor, not the state")

**Pitch.** Reverse sales/use-tax audits are classic contingency work at **30–40% of refunds** ([RK Miller](https://rkmillerassociates.com/sales-and-use-tax-compliance-consultants/salt-sales-and-use-tax-reverse-refunds-tax-recovery-services/)). Some charge up to 75%. The incumbents chase mid-market and enterprise clients:
- Agile Consulting, TaxMatrix, Cherry Bekaert, CLA, CBH, MGO;
- apexanalytix, Revenew and PRGX-type recovery firms

([TaxMatrix](https://www.taxmatrix.com/services/tax-recovery/), [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/industries/manufacturing-and-distribution/reverse-sales-tax-audit-for-manufacturing), [apexanalytix](https://www.apexanalytix.com/solutions/audit-recovery/sales-and-use-tax-recovery-audit/), [Revenew](https://revenew.com/services/sales-tax-use-tax-recovery)).

The *new* angle: agents OCR every AP invoice from a $2M–$30M manufacturer or contractor and flag tax charged on exempt purchases (manufacturing equipment, resale, SaaS in non-taxing states, freight shown separately). Recovery runs **through the vendor**: send an exemption certificate and a credit request, which avoids tax representation and the POA. A state refund claim is filed only through a partner CPA or tax firm.

**Data and inputs.** AP invoice PDFs and the vendor list, which are client-provided (a trust hurdle), plus state exemption rules (public).

**Buyer.** Controller or owner of small manufacturers, machine shops and contractors.

**Evidence that money moves.** The contingency market is long-established, and the firms above publish manufacturing-specific offers. Deloitte's CogTax already applies OCR and ML to "full population" invoice review ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2019/08/27/deloitte-transforms-indirect-tax-recovery-with-ai/34633/)).

**Market size (est.).** About 250k US manufacturing establishments **(unverified)**; perhaps 60k in the $2M–$30M band. If 10% hold more than $15k of recoverable tax over 3–4 years at a 30% fee, that is about $4.5k per client, or **~$27M** of first-pass revenue.

**Deliverable and pricing.** A refund and credit package at 30% of recovered amounts. A recurring add-on: a $99–$299/mo "tax-on-invoice monitor" that flags vendor overcharges going forward.

**Automation pipeline.**

## 4. Self-verification results

Two general-purpose sub-agents attacked the top ideas in parallel. The first took Idea 1 (about 30 searches). The second took Ideas 2–4 (about 27 searches). **Every idea came back WEAKENED; none was CONFIRMED.** Factual errors they found have been corrected inline above, each marked as a correction or retraction.

### Idea 1 (RateLift, non-auto warranty): WEAKENED, close to KILLED for equipment and marine

**Equipment statutes don't create the job.** Most farm, outdoor-power and construction statutes (AL, AR, GA, KY, MN, MS, NC, TN, VA, WY and others) pay the "established customer hourly retail labor rate". The only mechanism is that **the dealer notifies the supplier of its rate**. There is no 100-RO submission. Parts are fixed at **net + 15% minimum**, not a retail markup, so the draft's "$20k parts-markup uplift" per ag store is unsupported. KY and TN even allow the dealer to accept the supplier's terms instead ([EDA/Foley chart](https://farmequip.org/media/McGrath-State%20Statutes%20Handout.pdf)). The deliverable shrinks to a one-page letter.

**Marine OEMs often already pay the posted rate.** Garmin's 2026 marine policy pays the "published shop rate up to $190/hr" ([Garmin](https://www8.garmin.com/marine/PDF/marine-warranty-policy.pdf)). Marine statutes are a patchwork:
- VA pays the *lower* of the dealer's rate or the market rate;
- NY phases in to 100%;
- NE and SD require only "reasonable" compensation

([NMMA survey](https://www.nmma.net/assets/cabinets/Cabinet446/State%20Level%20Warranty%20Laws%20for%20Boat%20and%20Accessory%20Mfgs.pdf)).

**Per-store pools are small.** 2024 warranty claims paid were Polaris $151M, Harley $64M worldwide, Malibu $27M and MasterCraft $12M ([Warranty Week](https://www.warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20250424.html)). That implies roughly $3–10k a year of uplift per powersports or boat store, so a fee of about $1–3k, which can't carry a sales call (est., verifier).

**RV is the exception.** Thor paid $85.7M in claims in one quarter ([Warranty Week](https://www.warrantyweek.com/archive/ww20231207.html)). FL 320.696 ties RV warranty pay to retail charges ([FL](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/320.696)). Heavy truck is also live: SD's truck statute was amended in 2021 to require retail labor and parts ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/states-impose-new-requirements-dealer-warranty-reimbursement)). But in those segments the 100-RO mechanism exists only under the auto franchise acts, which is where Armatus, Wooden and Withum already operate.

**Adjacent competitor.** MarineLab, an MRAA member benefit, handles marine warranty *claims processing* for a percentage of paid claims ([MRAA](https://mraa.com/mraa-members-get-help-with-warranty-claims-through-marinelab/)). Lightspeed DMS submits OEM claims but has no rate-submission tool ([Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeeddms.com/solutions/service/)).

**Legal.**
- No UPL authority targets rate submissions.
- Anti-retaliation clauses were found in auto statutes but not in the equipment or marine statutes reviewed.
- *AEM v. Burgum* (2019) limits new equipment statutes' reach to existing contracts in 8th Circuit states ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca8/18-1115/18-1115-2019-08-02.html)).

**Verifier scores:** market 4, WTP 3, data 3, automation 6, competition 6, recurring 4, time-to-$ 3. **Overall about 4.**

**I accept this.** I narrow the surviving idea to **RV and heavy-truck dealers in states whose auto franchise act covers them** and score it 4.0.

**Retroactive "found money" is mostly fiction.** Pre-approval and 30–60-day windows are standard: Carrier requires claims within 60 days of the invoice with a 15 Dec hard cutoff ([Carrier Enterprise 2026](https://resource.carrierenterprise.com/is/content/Watscocom/ce_ca_carrier-program-benefits_pdf_20260304_2026_Co-Op_AdvertisingPolicy_Carrier-Janpdf)), and Lennox requires prior approval. What can be recovered is *unspent current-period accrual*.

**OEM gating is worse than the draft said.** It covers Yamaha, Honda, Kawasaki, and Lennox's Preferred Vendor Program ([Rynoss](https://rynoss.com/partners/lennox-preferred-vendor/)).

**Competitors added:**
- Dealer Spike: 100+ OEM programs, about $495–$2,000+/mo per third-party estimates ([Dealer Spike](https://www.dealerspike.com/partnership/honda/)).
- Ansira/BrandMuscle: from about $4,500/mo, sold to OEMs ([SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/p/marketing-automation-software/ansira/)).

**The "$14–35B unclaimed" figure** is a 2016 estimate from a media-sellers' trade group. Treat it as marketing, not a market size.

**My call:** keep it as an add-on that raises close rates and retention for the brothers' existing site and ads business. It is not a standalone found-money product.

### Idea 3 (vendor-side sales-tax recovery): WEAKENED, borderline kill, 4/10

**Vendor credits are voluntary.** In CA only the retailer can claim from the state, and it must first repay the customer ([Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2018/nov/retailer-not-required-file-sales-tax-refund-claim-consumer/), [CDTFA 6902](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6902.html)). **Competitor missed in the draft:** Arthiva (AI tool turning AP records into refund claims) ([arthiva.ai](https://arthiva.ai/)).

### Idea 4 (FICA tip credit, salons): WEAKENED, 4/10 → I lower it to 3.5

**The draft had the statute wrong.** It said there was a 15% tips/gross-receipts test; there is none ([26 USC 45B](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/45B)). The credit is nonrefundable ([PBA](https://www.probeauty.org/fica-tip-tax-faqs/)).

**There are no restaurant-style lookback refunds**, because only 2025 onward is claimable. That kills the "found money" framing.

**Barbershops are mostly ineligible:** 80% of barbers are self-employed ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/barbers-hairstylists-and-cosmetologists.htm)).

**Competitors added:**
- TippedRefund: 25% success fee; salons "coming".
- Boyum and Citrin Cooperman: targeting salons ([Boyum](https://myboyum.com/salon/fica-tip-credit-for-salons-spas-and-barbershops-in-2025/), [Citrin](https://www.citrincooperman.com/In-Focus-Resource-Center/OBBBA-Expands-FICA-Tip-Credit-Beyond-Restaurants)).

**Referral model:** California bans CPAs from paying a fee "to obtain a client" ([B&P 5061](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-5061/)). In Texas a CPA may pay with disclosure, but is liable for the non-CPA's acts ([TSBPA](https://www.tsbpa.state.tx.us/enforcement/faq.html)).

**Circular 230 date correction:** REG-116610-20 was proposed in Dec 2024 and is not finalized ([CPA Trendlines](https://cpatrendlines.com/2025/01/17/major-changes-to-circular-230-implications-for-tax-professionals-cornerstone-report/)).

### What the verification taught

The lane's base assumption fails twice over:
1. **Where recovery rights are statutory and recurring, the money per SMB is small.** Non-auto warranty, salon tip credit and settlements all show this.
2. **Where the money per client is large, incumbents and licensing already own it.** Auto warranty, reverse audits, R&D and public adjusting all show this.

The one usable piece is **co-op**, as a funding source for the brothers' existing product, not as a business of its own.
